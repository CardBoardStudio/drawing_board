New in this playtest 39.44 Playtest 2026-11-16: One rival policy for games and simulations View changes
  • Keep the original resource mat on the green table: The green background restores production squares and the gold capacity marker. The star background keeps the combined circle display with thick production rings; switching backgrounds updates the mat immediately. Resources and storage.
  • Choose your table background: Toggle Star background in the game menu to switch between green and stars. Your browser remembers your choice. Background.
  • Read battles across the card table: Enemy ships and tactics now face yours across a battle table, using the map’s familiar unit symbols, attack values and hull tracks. See both public tactic inventories, select a card, then commit your secret choice. Combat.
  • A clearer starfield: The optional star background has brighter stars in three sizes, more stars, and full-height coverage. Background preference.
  • One rival policy for games and simulations: New shared tables and AI arena matches use the same computer rivals as solo play. Existing tables retain their dealt policies. Computer rivals.
  • Compare build costs in a resource table: The map key shows ships, buildings, and excavation in rows with four resource columns. Blank cells mean zero cost. Ships and buildings.
  • Fleet protection and Buy exchanges: Fleets take combat hits before Settlers, followed by Explorers and Shipyards. Play held 2:1 exchange cards during your own Buy turn as well as Operations, and keep buying afterward. The Trash pile shows its price: 3 of one resource per card. Combat · Exchange cards · Buy.
  • Keep Capacity Modules at a fixed price: Every Capacity Module costs 1 Matter, 1 Energy, 1 Biomass and 1 Data. Buying or storing modules never increases the price. Capacity Modules.
  • Focus the Cheap market on draw and exchange cards: Cheap now contains only the three draw-two and three 2:1 exchange designs. Ion Thrusters, Slipstream Charts, Rail Battery and Deflector Grid move to Medium. Six other former Cheap designs leave the new-game supply. Prices and effects stay the same. Market rows.
  • Exchange cards, market prices and goal-directed rivals: Three cheap exchange cards let you choose a resource to trade at 2:1 until your Buy ends; eight market cards cost less and World Engine draws after its free build. New games default to 30 Prestige, and rivals improve production, purchase planning and goal-directed exchanges. Exchange orders, market cards, rival planning, exchange planning, victory target.
  • Play against MaxBot: Choose MaxBot in New game to replace the Economy rival. It seeks reusable capacity, develops Harvesters and Colonies with local Shipyards, and turns toward combat and Prestige purchases later. New games only. MaxBot.
  • Randomize your solo starting home: New solo games deal all four home-world positions at random, so you can start at any corner. The setup preview names your actual neighbor, and saved games keep their dealt positions. Solo setup.
  • Replace enemy Harvesters on captured worlds: After capturing a world’s Colony, use Construct and pay the normal Harvester cost to replace an enemy Harvester there with your own. Its hex and production become yours. Colonies, Shipyards and your own buildings cannot be replaced. Building.
  • Tiered gallery and unclaimed destroyed Shipyards: Browse the card gallery by Expensive, Medium and Cheap tiers, with sorting and deck filters inside each tier. Destroying a Shipyard now clears ownership of its hex, allowing a normal paid claim and build there again. Market tiers · Destroyed Shipyards.
  • Members dashboard for hosts: Administrators get a Members page beside Invitations: every invitation link with how many people accepted it and who, and every registered account with how they joined, when they were last seen and how many tables they have. Players see no change.
  • Capacity is now a card: Add 4 capacity is now the Capacity Module, a card you buy from the Upgrades tray during Buy for 1 + the cards in your Storage pile, of each resource (the table shares 20). It enters your discard like any purchase; play it during Operations as your action to add 4 capacity points where you choose, and it goes to your permanent Storage pile beside your mat. You can no longer buy capacity off turn. Resources & storage.
  • Card art from the National Gallery of Art: Every card now shows a public-domain painting, print or photograph from the National Gallery of Art, Washington: Romantic seascapes and landscapes for market and starter cards, naval battles for the military deck, and Piranesi engravings and star charts for the alien relics. On the table a market card's picture fills the space its text leaves. The card gallery lists every work and its artist. The art never changes what a card does (Card reference). The game menu, rulebook and card gallery end with the version and a short art notice.
  • Read a card from its corners: Every card shows what it does as symbols in its upper-left corner (one hammer on Construct, two on Construct II) and, in the upper-right corner, what a level I starter converts to or a market card's price. In your hand those corners are the card's buttons: press the symbols to use the order, the resource to convert. Your hand is a fan in which both corners of every card show; point at a card to raise it whole. Read a card from its corners.
  • A larger map key with prices beside their names: The map key's names and card icons are larger, each price sits just after its name, faint lines part the rows, and each resource's symbols form one cluster: two side by side, three as a small triangle. On an upright tablet the costs are in the Map key, not on the table. Shared board.

        Your turn

        Convert level I starters for resources, then use one order, offer a trade, or pass. Passing immediately starts your Buy turn. Finish Buy, then the remaining factions continue Operations.

        Your round

        Produce → Operations (pass → your Buy turn) → everyone finishes → cleanup → victory check → draw five → next round.

        Your deck

        Buy as many cards as you can afford during your Buy turn. Upgrade original starters to II for two of one resource, then III for three of one resource; III draws one card when used as an order. Only level I starters convert to resources. Trash eligible discarded cards, including starters, for three of one chosen resource type each. There is no one-card trash limit. Gained cards go to discard. Played and converted cards stay in play until shared cleanup. Buy a Capacity Module from the Upgrades tray (1 of each resource, every time); play it during Operations to add 4 capacity points, and it stays in your permanent Storage pile. Successful Colonize, Excavate and Cargo Expansion are permanently trashed. Probe keeps its combined card. Stored resources carry forward.

        Your goal

        Reach the table’s Prestige target (30 unless chosen otherwise when the game was created) at the end of a round. Colonies, excavated relics, and decisive combat victories contribute. Click a point total to see its breakdown. The rulebook includes costs, scoring, and current card clarifications.
