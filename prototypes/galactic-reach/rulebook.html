A GALAXY. FOUR FACTIONS. YOUR NEXT MOVE.

Make your mark
among the stars.

Explore new worlds, turn a small deck into an engine, and race to the table’s Prestige target: 30 unless your table chose 20, 40, 50 or 60. Expand, research, build, or bargain: you’ll need more than one route to the top.

Your first minute

  1. Look at your hand. Each card is a choice: convert it into resources, or use its order.
  2. Take one action. Convert any cards you need, then play one order or offer a trade. The next faction takes a turn.
  3. Keep taking turns until you pass. Passing starts your Buy turn immediately. Improve your deck, then let the remaining factions continue.
  4. Build toward Prestige. Colonies, excavated relics, decisive combat victories, and some market cards score Prestige. Victory is checked when everyone finishes the round.

Try this: save a Construct card for its order; convert another card to help pay for a Settler. You cannot use the same card both ways.

Learn to play. New to the game? Choose Learn to play on the home page or in the game menu. It deals a practice game in which the three rivals only pass, and teaches one step at a time on the real table, in the order of this manual. A note beside each part of the table lights it up; when there is something to do, it waits until you have done it, and otherwise you choose Next. Between lessons it may give you cards or resources, or move one of your units ahead, and the note and game log say so. Back, Skip lesson and Exit are always there, and your place is saved. The practice game is stored separately and never changes your guest game.

Guidance. In your own games, a note next to the card, hex or button it means suggests a useful next step on each of your turns, and explains each rule in one line at the moment it first happens (your first new hand, production, a reshuffle). Its suggested opening is a Data Harvester (2 Data every round, for Explorers, excavation and upgrades), then a Shipyard. The first time a rule matters (your first Buy turn, Shipyard, Colonize card, relic, full resource or battle), it explains that rule once. It changes no rule and makes no choice for you. Turn it off or back on with Guidance in the game menu.

This manual describes the 38.43 Construct III Playtest. It combines the Balance Package, aligned corner portals, and playtest feedback. Captures need a Fleet and Settler, and decisive combat victories score 2 Prestige. Manage resources off turn, choose Probe or Excavate on one card, and try level III starters, new relics and affordable draw cards. Start a new game for these rules. Earlier tables keep their archived engines and manuals.

01 / Your starting faction

Four factions share thirteen seven-hex worlds and four single-hex moons on a radius-10 galaxy (331 hexes). Four home worlds touch the rim. The two neutral corner worlds each sit one hex inward toward the center, so their outermost hexes stop one hex short of the rim. Six neutral worlds form a symmetric ring around a seventh world at the exact center. Every home has two neutral world centers six steps away. World positions stay fixed; neutral world identities and resource tiles are shuffled each game. All thirteen worlds, including their resources, ownership, and buildings, are visible to everyone from setup. Four moons use randomized positions spread across the map, with comparable travel from every home. Moons and relic locations start hidden.

At least one empty hex separates terrain. Each of the nine neutral worlds, including the center, and each moon has one relic: thirteen sites total. A site stays hidden until located; arriving at its world does not locate it. After excavation that site is gone. All worlds cover seven hexes; moons cover one and never produce resources.

You start with only a central Colony: no Settler, Explorer, Fleet, Shipyard, or other building. Your six starter cards are 3 Move and 3 Construct. There are no starting Colonize, Probe, or Excavate cards. Combat uses a separate four-card tactic hand, refreshed at the start of every battle.

Draw your opening hand at setup. Shuffle your six starters and draw five before Round 1 begins; the sixth card waits in your deck. After that, you draw each new hand at the end of a round (Cleanup & draw). Each faction starts with 1 Life and 1 Data (32.37, issue #41; it was 2 Life). Your Colony produces 1 Matter, so you open with 1 Matter, 1 Life and 1 Data. Build a Shipyard with Construct, then commission your first units. Use the 4:1 bank to obtain Data before you have an Excavate.

Your starting position and neighbor. Each new solo game randomly assigns the four factions to the four home-world positions. You can start at any of them, with an equal chance for each; your faction keeps its name and colour. You still take the first turn. Homes come in pairs: the left and upper homes share one half of the map, and the right and lower homes share the other half. The three computer rivals (Ember Directorate, Military; Violet Assembly, Economy; Helios Compact, Expansion) also keep their names, colours and profiles. New game names the rival sharing your half before you start, for example “Your neighbor: Violet Assembly (Economy)”, and the game log repeats it. Reloading or undoing never rerolls home positions; only starting another game deals them again. The Learn to play practice game keeps its fixed starting position. At a shared table, people are seated at random instead.

02 / A round of play

  1. Produce. Collect resources. You already hold the hand you drew at the end of the last round (or at setup).
  2. Operations. Starting with the first player, alternate one action: use an order, publish a trade, or pass. Converting eligible level I starters and bank exchanges are free during Operations or Buy, even while another faction acts. Orders and player trade responses still use their normal timing. Passing ends your Operations for this round and immediately starts your Buy turn.
  3. Buy. When you pass, take your own Buy turn before play moves on. Buy as many cards as you can afford, including Capacity Modules; you may also upgrade starters and decommission cards. Finish Buy to resume Operations with the next faction that has not passed.
  4. Clean up & draw. After the last faction finishes Buy, move everyone’s remaining hand and cards in play to discard, then check victory. If nobody wins, every faction draws its next five cards, the first-player marker rotates, and the next round begins. If someone wins, the game ends without a draw. Whenever your deck runs out, shuffle your discard pile to keep drawing.

Your next hand arrives at cleanup. You see it while the table finishes the round, so you can plan before Production. Nothing else moves: Production uses no cards, the cards you bought this round are already in your discard, and nobody can use the new hand until Operations. Unused reactions in it wait for a battle next round. For example, you finish Buy holding one Move, with a Construct in play and one card left in your deck. At cleanup, Move and Construct go to discard. You draw your last deck card, shuffle your discard (including this round’s purchases), and draw four more.

Newly gained cards always enter your discard pile. This includes purchased cards, unit-bundled Colonize and Probe cards, alien relic rewards. Draw them through normal deck circulation before using them. Drawing cards you already own and returning an existing card to hand follow their printed effects.

Keep used cards in play until the shared round cleanup. This includes cards used for an order, converted for resources, used during Buy, or triggered as reactions. They are outside your hand, deck, and discard, so later draws and discard-recovery effects cannot pick them up again this round. Your own Buy turn is the exception: you may upgrade a starter or trash a card that is in play. For example, converting Move and then playing a draw-three card cannot draw that Move, even if your deck empties. Only the discard pile reshuffles. At cleanup, discard your cards in play together with your remaining hand. An effect that explicitly discards, trashes, or returns a card is an exception: successful Colonize and Excavate orders are still permanently trashed, and Cargo Expansion is trashed after raising your limits. A played Capacity Module goes to your Storage pile, never to discard. The combined Probe / Excavate card stays in play after Probe and is trashed after successful excavation. Newly gained cards still enter discard. Keep unused reactions in hand until they trigger or cleanup occurs. Resources carry between rounds.

A card that raises one named resource limit leaves the other three unchanged. For example, Keepers’ Archive changes limits of 4/4/4/4 to 4/4/4/6, then draws three cards. Your limits and full report remain saved when you reload.

03 / Resources & storage

On the green background, your player mat keeps its original two rows: square Rate pips count production, filled Now circles count stock, and the gold circle just after the last usable slot marks capacity. Switch to Star background in the menu for the combined circle display: Small filled discs count the resources you hold. Each sits inside a dark slot, leaving a visible gap even when you have stock but no production. Resource-coloured rings count what your current planets, Harvesters, and combat fleets would produce each round; a producing circle is filled only when you also hold that resource. Faded circles mark slots beyond that resource’s limit, without a separate capacity marker. Production can exceed capacity: those rings still show the full production before automatic excess discards. For example, with 1 Matter held, production of 2, and a limit of 4, the first circle is filled and ringed, the second is empty and ringed, and slots after the fourth are unavailable. Exact stock, limit, and production are included in the row’s accessible label; values beyond the 21-circle display also appear as numbers. The display updates when control, buildings, or ships change. Enemy ships never stop the owner’s production. Explorers alone and moons produce nothing. Card conversions and one-time rewards are not included in production.

Matter builds; Energy funds fleets and cards; Life commissions Settlers and founds colonies; Data buys and improves cards. Pay the listed cost when you build or buy. Ordinary movement has no resource cost.

Life is the green leaf resource, previously called Biomass. Use it for Settlers, Colonies, and the other printed Life costs.

Start with four separate limits: 4 Matter, 4 Energy, 4 Life, and 4 Data. Unused space for one resource cannot hold another resource. For example, 5 Matter exceeds your starting Matter limit even if you have no other resources.

Raise your limits with Capacity Module cards. During your Buy turn, buy a Capacity Module from the capacity pile in the Upgrades tray. Like every gained card, it enters your discard pile. Once you draw it, play it during Operations as your action: assign 4 permanent capacity points among the four resource limits, then put the card in your Storage pile. Each point raises one chosen limit by 1. You may put all four into one resource, split them 2–2, add 1 to each, or use any other split totaling exactly four. For example, assigning 3 to Data and 1 to Energy changes your starting limits to 4 Matter, 5 Energy, 4 Life, and 7 Data. These are capacity points, not Prestige.

Your Storage pile is permanent. It lies beside your player mat and holds every Capacity Module you have played, each keeping the split you chose for it. It is never shuffled into your deck, drawn, discarded or trashed, and cleanup does not touch it. Your limits are 4 of each resource plus the points on your stored modules (and any capacity from relics and market cards).

A Capacity Module always costs 1 Matter, 1 Energy, 1 Life and 1 Data. The price never increases, no matter how many modules you buy or store. You may buy several in one Buy turn while you can afford them. The table shares one supply of 20 modules; when it is empty, nobody can buy more. Buying a module does not use or refill a market slot. Procurement Hub and Trade Syndicate discount market cards only, so a module neither benefits from nor uses up their discount. A module cannot convert to resources. You may trash one from your hand, discard or in play during Buy like any other card, but never from your Storage pile. Playing it opens a selector with 1 point on each resource. Edit that split if you wish, then confirm; cancelling plays nothing and keeps your action. For example, you buy your first module for 1 of each resource on round 2. You draw it on round 4, play it as your Operations action with 3 points on Data and 1 on Energy, and it goes to your Storage pile. Your next module still costs 1 of each, and so does every later module.

Only level I starter cards can convert to resources. These are your six original Move and Construct cards, before you upgrade them. A level I Move converts for 1 Energy; a level I Construct converts for 1 Matter. Level II and III starters, unit-bundled cards, market cards, Wonders, and relics cannot convert. An upgrade trades away the starter’s conversion option for a stronger order. Convert level I starters converts only eligible level I starters, from left to right, and leaves every other card in your hand. Automatically discard each resource’s excess after every conversion. Conversions spend no Operations action and never draw a card.

Whenever you gain resources, immediately discard the amount above each resource’s own limit. There is no discard prompt or waiting step. For example, with 3 Matter and a Matter limit of 4, a gain of 3 Matter keeps 1 and automatically discards 2. Space for another resource does not help. Production, eligible starter conversions, and bank gains follow this rule for every faction. The game log and report record what was gained and lost.

Capacity cards. Some market cards need raised limits. Signal Relay, Survey Network, and Tactical Briefing each draw two cards and can be bought within your starting limits of 4. This experiment also brings Strike Vector, Prefab Yards, Seed Ark, Colony Pods, Procurement Hub and Trade Syndicate within those limits; see their printed prices in the card reference. Raise the limits shown in the price before buying a card that needs more than 4. Several other market cards need 5 of one resource, so one Capacity Module is enough: Ion Thrusters costs 5 Energy. The strongest cards need more: Ascendancy Codex costs 8 Data, 4 Life and 2 Energy. A full limit only lets you hold the price; you still pay it during your Buy turn. For example, to buy Ion Thrusters, buy a Capacity Module (1 of each resource) on one Buy turn and play it on a later Operations turn with at least 1 point on Energy. Then hold 5 Energy when you buy. 32.37 prices are about 30% lower than 31.36 (376 resources across the set became 266).

Moons never produce resources. Rich planets and their star markers are removed. Each owned resource planet hex produces 1 of its resource. A Harvester produces 2 total of its hex’s resource (in 31.36 and earlier it produced 1, the same as any owned hex). Do not add a separate base resource to Harvester production. A combat fleet on a planet hex also earns its faction 1 of that resource, regardless of ownership. That is one bonus per fleet, not per ship; Explorers alone earn none.

You may convert level I starter cards or trade 4:1 with the bank during Operations or Buy while another faction acts. Capacity is not free: you buy a Capacity Module on your own Buy turn and play it as an Operations action. These actions affect only your resources and cards; they do not spend or change the active faction’s order. They pause during combat and mandatory modal choices. You may still fund an optional second Construct build. A full resource limit never disables conversion: excess is discarded automatically, even if the whole gain is lost. Converting your last card on your own Operations turn passes you to Buy immediately unless a second build or another mandatory choice is pending. Off-turn conversions never pass the active faction.

04 / Your basic orders

Point at a hex, or focus it with Tab, to see which cards in your hand could start an order there: those cards rise and glow in your hand, as a card does while you aim it. A hex where no order can start raises none. Select the hex (tap it on a touch screen) to open its Sector Scan beside it. It lists those orders, each with its card’s picture. Choosing one starts that order exactly as Use order or dragging the card onto the hex would. Escape or a click elsewhere closes the Sector Scan. Movement shortcuts select the card and fleet together; build and colonize shortcuts open the matching choices and costs. You still need the normal card, resources, and a legal target. The interface opens Hand when your Operations turn begins and Market when you pass into Buy; you can switch tabs yourself between these transitions. Pass & buy and Finish Buy sit beside your hand.

Turns with nothing to decide finish for you. This applies to solo and ordinary shared tables. New phone-paired tabletop games instead require confirmed Pass and Finish Buy (final choices). These shortcuts change no rule; each one makes the only remaining move, and Undo restores the turn (at a shared table the same shortcuts apply; you may rewind later bot turns until another human acts). If your Operations hand becomes empty after a conversion, or your Operations turn begins with no cards in hand and no trade offer is waiting for your answer, the table passes you to Buy. (You could still post a trade first: use Undo turn to get the turn back.) If your Buy turn then offers no choice at all, meaning no card you can afford, no starter to upgrade, no Buy effect, no card to trash, no Capacity Module you can afford and no bank exchange, Buy finishes automatically. A notice beside your hand, for example “No cards left: moved to Buy”, stays until you finish your next turn. Computer rivals and replays are unaffected.

05 / Exploration & movement

In the game menu, toggle Star background to switch between the green table and the earlier starfield. Your browser remembers the choice. It affects only your view.

Read shared table information around the board. On a desktop window the whole window is one table, shrunk to fit a small window but never grown past its natural size, so browser zoom makes the pieces larger or smaller and the map takes what is left. Left column, top to bottom: the standings, a small printed track racing every faction to the Prestige goal (one lane per faction, headed by its name, with its marker at its current score; the acting faction’s marker glows, with an arrow; point at or focus a marker for that faction’s Prestige sources, colonies, cards bought and fleet power, or select it for the breakdown), and beside each lane that faction’s public counts in a small table under one row of icons: Settlers, Explorers, Fleets, strongest fleet power, Colonies, Harvesters, Shipyards, excavated relics and cards bought (point at an icon for its name; on a phone the track and table are the Table drawer); the round’s phases; the comms feed as a small card of the latest entries (select it to open the whole feed, and again or press Escape to close it; each entry shows who acted, a symbol for the kind of event and its resource and Prestige amounts; point at an entry for the full sentence); and below them, in the middle of the column’s free height, the map key: the terrain colours, each piece’s price, the Excavate cost and the card icons (the Map key button has the full descriptions); notices appear over the top of the key. Each upgrade card shows its price with an icon for each resource you could pay with, lit when your stock can pay it now. All are drawn as real cards: name and effect from the top-left and the cost in one box sharing the card’s top-right corner (an upgrade’s box shows its price over the resources it may be paid with); on a tall window they take real card proportions, on a shorter one they stay wide enough to read whole. Card effects use a shared set of icons: ★ Prestige, a card for Draw, an arrow for Move, crossed swords for a Hit, a shield for a Miss (a hit prevented), a gold pip for a resource limit, a flag for Colonize and a hammer for Build; the full sentence shows when you point at a card (choose it to buy), and cards you can buy now are outlined in gold. Along the bottom: the menu and your Deck, In play and Military piles, your hand, Convert level I starters (shown only while you hold one you may convert) and Trade left of a large player mat, and Discard with the turn’s main button just above it (on a squarer window, all three buttons stack above Discard). There is no written turn prompt on the table: your glowing hand shows it is your turn (screen readers still hear what to do). The game’s title, rules, card gallery, Preview all alien relics, new game, Undo, playtest notes, Learn to play and the Guidance switch open from the menu button in the bottom-left corner. The recent-action feed can say that Probe was used, but never gives its private relic location. Your hand, card piles, reserves, and full game-report download stay on your player mat. World terrain is always public, but seeing a world does not locate its relic. Any Settler, Fleet, or Explorer ending a move surveys that world or moon, revealing its terrain to everyone. Arrival never locates a relic. Use Probe from your Explorer’s current hex to locate the nearest remaining relic you have not already discovered, even on an unvisited world or hidden moon. Known sites are skipped. When you locate the last unknown site, a message tells you that all remaining relics are discovered. If all remaining sites are already known to you, Probe warns you without spending the card or your action. You do not need to reach that world or moon first. A signal on an undiscovered moon reveals the relic’s hex to you without revealing the moon’s terrain. Other players must locate it for themselves. Existing discovery relic cards keep their printed effects. Probe and Chorus signals stay private after later terrain surveys. Only an effect that explicitly reveals relics to everyone makes them public. All players’ units remain visible, including in empty space. Unit markers alone do not reveal a hidden moon or relic.

Read each ship type separately on the map. A number beneath a ship icon is the count of that type; an icon without a number means one. A building icon is separate from the ships. Select the hex to read every faction’s exact units in Sector Scan. On a narrow screen, pan the map or use Zoom map for a closer view.

Play a Move order on one hex where you have units, then choose which of them go. Choose any number of your Settlers and Fleets there, or any number of your Explorers. At first every combat ship on the hex is chosen (or every Explorer, if you have no combat ship there). Untick units, or lower a count, to leave them behind. Use Tab to reach these checkboxes and count selectors; changing a choice keeps focus on the same control. The units you leave stay where they are.

The chosen units move together, up to the speed of the slowest one. Settlers have 1 movement, Fleets 2 movement, and Explorers 3 movement. Each ordinary hex step uses 1 movement. Move II adds 1 movement to that order, and a movement card adds its range to the chosen units. Combat ships and Explorers never move in the same order: Explorers travel as their own civilian team and use Excavate to recover relics. Each Move order moves one chosen group from one hex. Moving the units you left behind takes another Move order, on a later turn.

Example: you have 1 Settler and 2 Fleets on a hex. Moving all three goes 1 hex, because the Settler is slowest. Untick the Settler and the two Fleets move 2 hexes while the Settler stays. Or move one Fleet 2 hexes and leave the other Fleet guarding the Settler. Two Explorers on one hex can head for two different relics with two Move orders.

There is no limit to the number of your ships on one hex. Fleets, Settlers, and Explorers may share it in any quantity, within their faction-wide component limits. The same stacking rule applies when commissioning, escaping, and retreating. A forced retreat and a paid Explorer escape are not Move orders: a retreat takes every surviving attacker, and an escape follows the combat rules.

Enemy ships and enemy Shipyards make a hex hostile. Enemy ownership alone does not: cross or stop on an undefended enemy Colony or resource hex normally, with no battle or seizing message. Entering a hostile hex stops the movement and resolves combat. You may spend a Move order to stay on your current hex (zero movement). If enemy ships or an enemy Shipyard remain there, this starts another battle; the card and action are spent normally. A peaceful stay still spends them. You cannot move through it. Pathfinder Network avoids hostile hexes; other orders may enter them.

Cross the corner portal

The marked lower-left and upper-right corners are joined by a public portal. Five boundary hexes along each side share the corner hex: nine entrances at each end. Each entrance leads to the aligned hex straight across at the other corner, keeping their order. Select an entrance to see its paired exit.

Use a Move order and choose your ships normally. An ordinary step costs 1 movement; crossing the portal costs 2 movement from the entrance to its paired exit. You may walk to an entrance, cross, and keep moving in the same order if the chosen group has enough movement. Pay no resources to cross. For example, a Fleet starting on an entrance can cross with its normal Move. An Explorer can walk one step to an entrance and cross with its 3 movement. A base Settler needs Move II or another range bonus to cross.

Enemy ships or a Shipyard at the exit stop your move and start combat normally. A card that avoids hostile hexes cannot enter a hostile exit. Seeing a portal never reveals a hidden moon or relic. Retreats and Explorer escapes still use ordinary adjacent hexes; they do not cross the portal. Probe still measures ordinary hex distance to find the nearest relic.

06 / Ships, buildings & relics

Pay the base cost below when you build or excavate. A blank cell means none of that resource is required. Founding a Colony also consumes a Settler; excavation consumes an Explorer. Capturing an enemy Colony costs 1 Life instead of the 3 Life founding cost and needs a Fleet alongside your Settler. A card with a printed free or reduced Colony cost keeps that cost.

A Colony reserves its world for expansion; it does not immediately give ownership or production on every hex. Use Construct to claim an empty adjacent planet hex with a building. One building fits on a hex. Enemy presence does not remove the owner’s building permissions. A Colony cannot be replaced with a different building. All buildings have one level. Harvesters, Shipyards, and Colonies cannot be upgraded.

Reading ownership. A world belongs to whoever holds its center Colony; a moon belongs to whoever owns its Shipyard. The map rings each held world or moon in its owner’s faction colour. Every building is drawn in its own owner’s colour, with no disc behind it, so the hex’s resource colour stays visible. Neutral planets have no ring. After a capture, the ring takes the capturer’s colour, but surrounding buildings keep their owners’ colours. For example, if you capture Ember Directorate’s home Colony, its world turns your colour while Ember’s Harvester there stays orange. The terrain keeps its resource colour underneath.

Commission all units at a Shipyard you own. If enemy ships are on that hex, the new ship immediately starts a battle as attacker alongside your other ships there. Pay and gain the bundled card before combat; the Construct card is spent once. Your Shipyard stays on its hex when its newly commissioned ships attack; only a Shipyard participating as a defending piece can be destroyed. Construct II or III waits until combat ends before offering its optional second build. Commission Explorers only at an Shipyard you own. Colonies and other structures cannot commission them.

Excavate a relic

Browse the face-up rewards in the Relics tab, beside Hand, Market, Trade, and Fleet. First locate the relic with Probe or a discovery relic card. Then move your Explorer onto its exact hex and play Excavate. An unlocated relic cannot be excavated. Pay 1 Energy and 3 Data. Choose one of the three face-up alien relic cards and put it in your discard pile. Remove the relic from the map, gain 2 permanent Prestige, and immediately trash the used Excavate card. Research Stations are removed. Moons are gray and produce no resources even with ships or a Shipyard. 07 / Combat & conquest

Choose a tactic in each of up to three volleys. Every battle starts each side with 2 Concentrated Fire (+1 hit) and 2 Defensive Screen (prevent 2 hits), plus every card in that faction’s military deck. These tactics are separate from your regular deck. Every ship type can use any tactic.

Military deck. Military cards you buy from the market form your military deck. They never enter your hand, draw pile, or discard, and they cannot be converted or trashed. In each battle, each military card you own is one extra tactic you may choose once. A used tactic returns for your next battle. There is no shuffle, draw, or replacement. For example, with one Rail Battery you hold 2 Fire, 2 Screen, and 1 Rail Battery: five tactics for up to three volleys. Military cards are bought from the public market, so everyone can see which ones each faction owns and which tactics it has used; only the tactic just committed stays secret until both sides reveal. On the table your military deck is its own pile, Military, beside In play. It counts the military cards you own; during your battle it counts the ones you have not used yet. Select it to see each card and the two basic tactics. Military card effects are listed in the Card reference.

Escape before combat

Before either side chooses its first tactic, the attacker and then the defender may move some or all of their Explorers to one adjacent hex free of enemy ships and enemy Shipyards. Pay 1 Energy per escaping Explorer. The destination must be on the map. There is no ship-stacking limit. Ships left behind fight. Without enough Energy or a safe destination, choose to stay. This is the only paid Explorer escape window. If every defender escapes and no defending Shipyard remains, the attacker takes the hex without a volley.

Commit, reveal, resolve

Each side privately commits one of its available tactics. You can see whether your opponent has committed, but never their choice before you commit. Once both have chosen, reveal both cards and remove them from this battle’s hand. A used card cannot be chosen again. Public used tactics and remaining card counts help you plan later volleys. Each new battle restores all four cards.

Add the attack of all your surviving pieces, your chosen tactic, and applicable card bonuses. Subtract the opponent’s shields to find the damage they receive, to a minimum of zero. Phase Lance is the exception: the opponent’s shields prevent none of its hits that volley. Both sides deal damage simultaneously, using the pieces alive at the start of the volley. Apply hits to Fleets first, then Settlers, then Explorers, then Shipyards. Within a type, hit the piece with the least remaining hull first. For example, a Fleet with 7 hull absorbs the first 7 hits before a nearby Settler takes any, even if the Settler has only 2 hull. Damage stays on pieces between volleys. For example, one Fleet using Concentrated Fire deals 3 + 1 − 2 = 2 damage to an opponent using Defensive Screen. Reactions held in your regular hand still trigger automatically at their printed timing.

If either side has no pieces left, the battle ends. If defenders survive the third volley, the surviving attacker must retreat to an adjacent, enemy-free hex preferring its origin when legal. If no safe adjacent retreat exists, those attacking survivors are destroyed. Emergency Jump may end a battle earlier as described in the card reference; there is no automatic retreat based only on relative attack strength. Surviving pieces repair fully after the battle. Combat pauses normal play, including during a rival’s turn, until all choices are resolved; reloading resumes the pending choice. The moving order is spent once and its Operations turn ends once, after the battle. Review the automatic final report before normal play resumes. Excess resources are discarded automatically before the report. Reloading also preserves a report awaiting acknowledgment.

Decisive combat victory: gain 2 permanent Prestige when your side survives a fight and destroys every opposing unit that took part. Either the attacker or defender may earn it. The enemy must have brought at least one Settler, Explorer, or Fleet; destroying an undefended Shipyard alone earns no combat Prestige. If any opposing unit escapes or retreats, gain nothing, even if you destroy the units left behind. A mutual wipe earns neither side Prestige. Enemy survivors destroyed because no retreat is possible count as destroyed. Each fight awards at most one decisive victory, worth 2 Prestige (1 in 33.38 and earlier).

Combat does not change ownership or block production. There are no occupation or seizing markers. A Colony remains its owner’s, including its Prestige, until you capture it. To capture, you need a Settler and at least one of your Fleets on the enemy Colony’s center, with no enemy ships or enemy Shipyard there. Play Colonize and pay 1 Life. Consume the Settler; the Fleet stays. Trash the used Colonize card. A Settler alone cannot capture, and neither can a card that colonizes on its own terms (Seed Ark, Colony Pods, Genesis Charter, Genesis Seed Vault): each needs the Fleet too. For example, your Settler and Fleet end a Move together on Ember Directorate’s undefended home center; next turn, Colonize captures it and the Fleet stays to guard it. A lone Settler on that center can only wait for an escort. Only the center changes owner; surrounding buildings stay with their owners. The captured Colony gains a conquest marker.

08 / Trade

Open Bank exchange & discards beneath your resources. Choose one resource button under Give 4 and one under Receive 1, then click Convert. Selecting buttons spends nothing. Convert becomes available when the two types differ, you hold at least four of the resource to give, and no combat or mandatory choice is waiting. Keep the selections to repeat the exchange while you can pay.

Bank exchange, 4:1. During Operations or Buy, even off turn, give four of one resource to gain one of a different resource. For example, 4 Energy can become 1 Data; 2 Energy plus 2 Matter cannot. Repeat while you can pay. Bank exchanges are free, require no card or rival. They do not use your once-per-round player trade.

Exchange Protocol, Barter Network, and Clearing House. Play one from your hand during your Operations action or your own Buy turn, and choose the resource you will give. In Operations it uses your action. During Buy it is free to play and your Buy turn continues: you may exchange, purchase, upgrade, or trash cards afterward. You may play several held copies. A newly purchased copy enters discard and cannot be played immediately. Until you finish your Buy turn that round, give 2 of that resource for 1 of a different resource at the bank. Repeat while you can pay; each received resource follows its own storage limit. The choice also works between your Operations actions and off turn when normal bank trading is allowed. It changes no rival’s rate. The card stays in play and goes to discard at round cleanup. Playing another copy may improve a different resource; choosing the same one again never makes the rate better than 2:1. Your unused choices expire when your Buy turn ends, including an automatically skipped Buy. For example, choose Life, then exchange 2 Life for 1 Data twice if you hold 4 Life. Other resources still trade at 4:1.

Trade Consortium is a card order with a better rate: choose a free resource or up to two 2:1 exchanges. You choose every resource; see the card reference.

Once per round, use your Operations action to offer 1–5 of one resource for 1–5 of a different resource. You need the offered resources, but they are not reserved. At least one rival must still be taking Operations turns.

On their own turn, each rival may accept or reject for free. The first valid acceptance settles the offer; both sides must still have the resources and enough storage after exchanging. You may withdraw on your turn, but cannot post a second offer that round. Passing does not withdraw your open offers: rivals still in Operations may accept them, if the live balances allow it. All open offers expire when the last faction passes, before that faction’s Buy turn.

Game reports

Click Export game report beside the Game log to download a JSON file at any time, including after victory. It contains the saved position, rules version, faction and computer-rival profiles, resources, point breakdowns, and a chronological log with readable text.

13 / Current playtest clarifications

Computer rivals pursue Prestige with the same cards, costs, and legal actions as you. They plan routes through the public corner portal and account for visible enemies that can cross it; their movement pays its 2-point cost. They take available Colony and excavation opportunities before adding infrastructure. Every profile sends Settlers toward neutral Colony sites. Explorers use the Probe option from their current hex, retain the combined card, then travel to the privately located relic. They do not wait on a world or moon to search it. Fleets seek favorable fights and open enemy Colonies for capture. A Settler heads for an enemy center only with a Fleet in its group or already waiting there, because a capture needs both. A Fleet that needs its full speed may leave its Settlers behind, but only when those Settlers would be safe from nearby enemy Fleets on their own. Explorers pursue located relics and wait for Probe if no signal is known; they stop when the shared relic rewards are exhausted. Rivals see public terrain and units and their own private relic signals. They cannot consult hidden moon positions, unlocated relic sectors, enemy hands, or deck order. At a shared table the rivals are the AI arena’s reference policies: each rival seat plays the military, economic or expansion profile assigned to that seat; they follow the same rules and see the same limited information.

Rivals keep useful Move and Construct cards in circulation and preserve Colonize, Probe, and Excavate for their orders. Like you, a rival probes first, then uses a later draw of the combined card to excavate. They may skip a purchase, Capacity Module, or extra building to keep cards and resources for scoring. During Buy, rivals use the same in-play options you have: they may upgrade a starter that is in play, and trash a surplus basic card that was in their hand, discard or in play when their Buy began, with the same priorities wherever the card is.

Rivals plan storage using their current resources and the production their public ships and structures would earn. They keep payment for their next Colony and excavation, and for a first Shipyard when needed. They save a feasible mix for a worthwhile storage purchase and can buy capacity during Operations or before other Buy spending. They may buy before converting a card that would overflow. They shorten their planning horizon as a faction approaches victory and skip expensive upgrades unlikely to repay their cost. Forecasts use no hidden cards or future enemy decisions. Rivals pay the same fixed price of 1 of each resource for every Capacity Module. Their conversions lose excess of each resource automatically, just like yours. They can convert cards between Construct II’s two builds and may finish after one.

They avoid destinations exposed to nearby stronger Fleets, gather Fleets, escort threatened Settlers, and move toward safer hexes when outmatched. They upgrade Construct starters early and invest in missing Data production. They reinforce against visible threats and keep seeking captures when no neutral worlds remain. Military, Economy, and Expansion profiles favor different routes, but all pursue points. Their first Shipyard still uses the inward Energy hex, and equal-distance paths use their home’s orientation. A blocked or illegal move costs no card. The rival may then choose another action or convert a card normally.

Rivals can upgrade their six original starters through level III. They protect payment for an immediate Colony, excavation, or urgent defensive Fleet. They compare additional Harvester production with the resources they need and the time left in the race, and compare worthwhile market purchases with further starter upgrades. A market-focused rival gives useful purchases and their required capacity earlier priority after its first Construct II. Only level I starters can fund resource conversions; a level III starter draws only when its order is used. A rival may finish an optional second construction after one build, paying the normal cost if it builds again.

Rivals watch public enemy Fleets near their Colony centers, including approaches through the portal. They can commission guards at nearby Shipyards and move reinforcements to a threatened center before pursuing a distant opportunity. They avoid leaving a defended center exposed. Economy and Expansion rivals also build defensive Fleets. They use public units and their own information; they never read an opponent’s hand, private relic signals, or deck order.

Card-ranking simulations only. A configured headless study may offer every remaining market design instead of only the nine face-up cards. It still pays the printed cost and takes a real copy from the shared supply. Each design has two copies; when both are bought, nobody can buy another. The study shows designs and remaining counts, never the hidden order of a deck. Ordinary solo and shared games keep the three-row market. Four identical market-focused rivals use the same legal rules and starting resources. Card-ranking tests may let one rival decline a specified design while all other choices remain available.

Buying and exchange timing. Current rivals compare printed card benefits, price, and the wait to draw a purchase from discard. For bank trades, they choose a concrete goal: a ready Colony or excavation, a useful build, a worthwhile available card, or a justified Capacity Module. They plan the whole payment using resources already held and current limits. They prefer immediate scoring, protect ready scoring payments when shopping, and weigh the resources lost to trading. A reserve shortage alone is not a reason to exchange. They enable a 2:1 rate only when it improves a funded plan, and skip it when the goal is already affordable or an existing rate is better. They reconsider after every action; future production is not current stock. These are planning preferences. Legal actions, costs, card effects, and exchange expiry stay the same.

Rivals value printed effects, price, available units and opportunities, and the wait to draw a new purchase from discard. They consider free or discounted builds, movement range, Colony terms, drawing power, useful capacity, Buy coupons, and Prestige. A discount is useful only when a later purchase uses it. They limit redundant copies, preserve named market and relic cards when trimming basic orders, and consider public opportunities to fight before buying more military cards. They buy Capacity Modules toward worthwhile prices above their limits while protecting ready scoring and defense payments. A distant expensive card does not automatically outrank one they can use sooner. They may keep buying useful, affordable cards during the same Buy turn. Browser and server rivals use their respective policies and the same legal choices and printed costs as you.

With Archive Purge, both kinds of rival keep at least 3 Move-order and 2 Construct-order cards and trash up to two level I starters beyond that, otherwise none. They take the second build of Prefab Yards as they do Construct II’s. Server rivals read printed Wonder Prestige, military card stats and the capture rule from the table they play, so an older table keeps its own values.

In a solo game, equivalent Move cards offer the rival the same routes. If their best moves tie, it keeps the first card in its hand. Building sites with the same price use the same payment choices from its current resources and eligible level I starters. After a move, payment, trade, draw, or other action changes the position, the rival considers the updated position before choosing again. Faster planning never grants extra movement, cheaper payments, or a new random choice.

These are recorded behaviors, not new rules introduced by this manual. Resolving them requires a separate rules change and playtest.

Retreats and civilian combat
  • Attacking survivors that cannot retreat are destroyed, matching the report.
  • Explorers and Settlers may attack. Both contribute 1 attack and use either basic tactic. War-Song and Obelisk’s offensive bonus require a surviving Fleet.
  • Combat damage targets Fleets, then Settlers, then Explorers, then Shipyards; within a type, hit the piece with the least remaining hull first. The old power-ratio withdrawal and random military draw pile are removed; the 31.36 military deck is a fixed set of owned tactics.
  • Purchased storage capacity is permanent. Combat never reduces it. Every gain automatically loses only the amount above each resource’s own limit; no discard decision interrupts play.
