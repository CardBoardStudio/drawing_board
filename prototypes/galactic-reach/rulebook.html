A GALAXY. FOUR FACTIONS. YOUR NEXT MOVE.

Make your mark
among the stars.

Explore new worlds, turn a small deck into an engine, and race to 20 Prestige. Expand, research, build, or bargain: you’ll need more than one route to the top.

Your first minute

  1. Look at your hand. Each card is a choice: convert it into resources, or use its order.
  2. Take one action. Convert any cards you need, then play one order or offer a trade. The next faction takes a turn.
  3. Keep taking turns until you pass. Passing starts your Buy turn immediately. Improve your deck, then let the remaining factions continue.
  4. Build toward Prestige. Colonies, fleets, Research Stations, and purchases all help. Victory is checked after the round’s resource directive.

Try this: save a Construct card for its order; convert another card to help pay for a Settler. You cannot use the same card both ways.

This manual describes the 11.16 Alien Relics experiment. Start a new game to try these rules. Older saved tables keep their own rules. Some printed card summaries are ahead of the implementation; the reference below records what those cards currently do.

01 / Your starting faction

Four factions share eleven seven-hex worlds on a radius-12 galaxy (469 hexes). Four home worlds touch the rim; seven inner worlds are randomized with at least one empty hex between worlds. Home worlds are visible to everyone.

You start with only a central Colony: no Settler, Explorer, Fleet, Shipyard, or other building. Your six starter cards are 3 Move and 3 Construct. There are no starting Colonize or Probe cards. Each faction also has its own shuffled 12-card military deck.

Production happens before the first five-card draw. Your Colony produces 1 Matter, so you open with 1 Matter and 2 Biomass. Build a Shipyard with Construct, then commission your first units. Use the 4:1 bank or round-end resource directives to obtain Data before you have a Probe.

02 / A round of play

  1. Produce, then draw five. Collect resources, then draw cards. Whenever your deck runs out, shuffle your discard pile to keep drawing.
  2. Operations. Starting with the first player, alternate one action: use an order, publish a trade, or pass. Converting cards and answering trades are free on your own Operations turn. Passing ends your Operations for this round and immediately starts your Buy turn.
  3. Buy. When you pass, take your own Buy turn before play moves on. Normally buy up to one card; you may also upgrade starters, decommission, and exchange relics. Finish Buy to resume Operations with the next faction that has not passed.
  4. Clean up. After the last faction finishes Buy, discard everyone’s remaining hand, check storage, and update public awards.
  5. Choose a directive, then check victory. The first player chooses a benefit for everyone. If nobody wins, rotate the first-player marker and start the next round.

Played and converted cards go straight to discard. Resources carry between turns and rounds; keep unused cards until the shared cleanup, so automatic reactions can still protect you after your Buy turn.

03 / Resources & storage

Matter builds; Energy funds fleets and cards; Biomass founds colonies; Data buys and improves cards. Pay the listed cost when you build or buy. Ordinary movement has no resource cost.

Start with space for 10 resources total. During your own Operations or Buy turn, pay 2 Matter, 1 Energy, and 1 Data to permanently add 5 storage. Repeat while you can afford it. This is a free resource-management action: it needs no card, map hex, building, or purchase, and preserves your current order. Occupation does not reduce this capacity. A card conversion needs room for its entire gain.

Convert all to resources checks your hand from left to right, converts every card whose full gain fits, and leaves the rest in hand. It includes Buy and reaction cards, so save those by converting individually instead. Converting a selected order cancels that selection; conversions never spend your Operations action.

Automatic gains that exceed storage are lost; when several types arrive together, storage fills in Matter → Energy → Biomass → Data order. During your own Operations or Buy turn, you may discard any number of stored resources to make room. Discarding is free, gives nothing in return, and does not spend a card or end your turn.

Each owned, unoccupied planet hex produces 1 of its resource, +1 if Rich, plus its Harvester level. A combat fleet on a planet hex also earns its faction 1 of that resource, regardless of ownership. That is one bonus per fleet, not per ship; Explorers alone earn none.

You still need the normal card, resources, and a legal target. The interface opens Hand when your Operations turn begins and Market when you pass into Buy; you can switch tabs yourself between these transitions.

05 / Exploration & movement

★ Rich: +1 production◈ Located relic◇ Researched relic

A Settler entering a world reveals all seven hexes to everyone. Probe signals are private. Settlers can explore without a Probe. Explorers can travel to a marked relic without revealing the surrounding world.

Select a group at one hex: all its combat ships, or its Explorer team. Move up to the slowest unit’s speed. Settlers move 1 hex, Fleets 2 hexes, Explorers 3 hexes. Move II adds 1 hex to that order. A group uses its slowest unit’s speed: a Settler and Fleet together move 1 hex. Explorers travel as a separate civilian team and establish Research Stations at relics. You cannot split individual ships from the selected group. Your destination may contain at most five of your units, including Explorers.

Entering a hostile hex stops the movement and resolves combat. You cannot move through it. Pathfinder Network avoids hostile hexes; other orders may enter them.

06 / Ships, buildings & relics

A Colony reserves its world for expansion; it does not immediately give ownership or production on every hex. Use Construct to claim an empty adjacent planet hex with a building. One building fits on a hex. Occupied hexes cannot build. A Colony cannot be replaced with a different building.

Commission all units at an unoccupied Shipyard you own. Commission Explorers only at an unoccupied Shipyard you own. Colonies and other structures cannot commission them.

Costs use the same colored symbols as your cards and resource bar. Each symbol is labeled, so you can learn them as you play.

The shuffled alien relic deck has 12 unique cards, one of each. Stations award cards immediately; there is no stored Relic inventory, three-recovery requirement, or Relic exchange action. Browse the alien relic cards below or use the in-game preview.

07 / Combat & conquest

Combat resolves automatically in up to three volleys. Each side deals one base hit per four surviving power, rounded up, then reveals a military tactic. Tactics add hits, shields, piercing, or withdrawal. Damage is simultaneous; hits go to the piece with the least remaining hull. Surviving units repair after the battle.

Each military deck contains 3 Concentrated Fire (+1 hit), 2 Defensive Screen (2 shields), 2 Flanking Run (+2 hits with a Settler or Fleet, otherwise +1), 2 Heavy Barrage (+2 with a Shipyard, otherwise +1), 1 Electronic Warfare (+1 hit, 1 shield, ignore 1 enemy shield), and 2 Tactical Withdrawal (1 shield, then withdraw if able). Used tactics reshuffle when exhausted. Reactions in hand trigger automatically.

If defenders remain after the battle, the attacker attempts to retreat to a non-hostile adjacent hex. Tactical Withdrawal and a badly outmatched attacker can end combat earlier. Fleet Overdrive prevents the attacker’s early withdrawal. Explorers contribute no base power and can be destroyed.

Winning at an enemy-owned planet occupies it, stopping its owner’s production. Ownership does not transfer. To capture its central Colony, later play Colonize there with a Settler and 1 Biomass. Only the center changes owner; surrounding structures do not transfer. The captured Colony gains a conquest marker.

08 / Trade

Bank exchange, 4:1. During your own Operations or Buy turn, give four of one resource to gain one of a different resource. For example, 4 Energy can become 1 Data; 2 Energy plus 2 Matter cannot. Repeat while you can pay. Bank exchanges are free, require no card or rival. They do not use your once-per-round player trade.

Trade Consortium is a card order with a better rate: choose a free resource or up to two 2:1 exchanges. You choose every resource; see the card reference.

Once per round, use your Operations action to offer 1–5 of one resource for 1–5 of a different resource. You need the offered resources, but they are not reserved. At least one rival must still be taking Operations turns.

On their own turn, each rival may accept or reject for free. The first valid acceptance settles the offer; both sides must still have the resources and enough storage after exchanging. You may withdraw on your turn, but cannot post a second offer that round. Passing does not withdraw your open offers: rivals still in Operations may accept them, if the live balances allow it. All open offers expire when the last faction passes, before that faction’s Buy turn.

09 / Improve your deck

As soon as you pass, buy up to one Move or Construct card, or one of the five face-up advanced cards. Colonize and Probe are bundled only with unit commissions. You cannot return to Operations this round. Purchases go to discard and refill the market. Basic supply is unlimited; the advanced deck has two copies of each card and recycles the market discard when needed.

During the same Buy turn you may also:

  • Upgrade starters: spend 1 Data per original starter in your hand or discard to make it level II. Purchased basic cards cannot upgrade.
  • Decommission cards: spend 1 Data per card to permanently trash as many cards as you can afford from your discard when your Buy turn began, including an original or upgraded starter. Cards still in hand, newly purchased cards, and Buy cards discarded during this turn are ineligible.
  • Use Buy cards. These do not consume the normal purchase. Hold Buy cards in hand until this phase; conversions are only available in Operations.

10 / Round-end resource directives

This experiment has no influence tracks or policies. Building, trading, buying, and upgrading do not award influence; there are no policy votes, policy discounts, or influence Prestige.

After every round, the first player chooses one resource directive. The chooser receives its primary benefit and each rival receives its lesser benefit. Storage limits apply. Resolve the directive, check victory, and then rotate the first-player marker.

11 / Prestige & victory

Score your current holdings plus permanent purchase milestones:

SourcePrestige
Colonies1 each, plus 1 per complete pair after the first Colony. Five Colonies score 7.
Shipyards1 per complete pair, regardless of level.
Research Stations1 per Research Station you established at a relic.
Purchased cards1 per eight lifetime purchases; scrapping a card does not remove this score.
Fleet Supremacy2 for the strongest single fleet, at least 8 power.
Galactic Reach1 for owning planet hexes in the most worlds, at least three worlds.
Occupation Mandate2 for owning the most conquest-marked Colonies, at least one.

Awards can change hands. A tied leader keeps an award they already hold; otherwise a tie leaves it unclaimed. Occupation alone does not remove ownership-based Prestige.

After the directive, if anyone has at least 20 Prestige, the highest Prestige wins. Break ties by most Colonies, then most stored resources. An exact tie selects the earliest seat in the browser; arena standings share that rank. Reaching 20 earlier does not lock in a win: finish the round before checking the standings. There is no round limit.

AI arena games use this same victory check. Only completed games earn placement ratings. A simulation that stops before victory awards no ratings.

Automatic reactions

Keep these in hand to use them; converting one spends it. Targeting Algorithms adds 1 hit and Ablative Plating prevents 2 hits in a volley, each consuming one available copy. Additional copies can trigger in later volleys.

Emergency Jump applies only to an attacker with surviving power below 55% of the defender’s after a volley: suffer one extra hit, then attempt withdrawal. It cannot interrupt Fleet Overdrive. Salvage Crews grants Matter equal to destroyed pieces, up to three, after combat; both factions can use it.

13 / Current playtest clarifications

These are recorded behaviors, not new rules introduced by this manual. Resolving them requires a separate rules change and playtest.

Occupation, retreats, and civilian combat
  • Occupation markers currently persist after the occupying fleet leaves. They continue to block production and building.
  • If a retreat cannot be completed, the combat report says the fleet was lost, but the current engine leaves survivors on the contested hex.
  • Explorers have zero base power, but a Explorer-only side can still receive hits and shields from tactics. Do not treat civilians as immune to combat.
  • Combat damage ties target Explorers, Settlers, Fleets, then Shipyards. A surviving attacker below 65% of defending power after the second volley attempts withdrawal unless using Overdrive.
  • Purchased storage capacity is permanent. Occupation and combat never reduce it; automatic gains and card conversions still respect the current capacity.
