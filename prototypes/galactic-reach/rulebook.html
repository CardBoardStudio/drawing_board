HEIRS AD ASTRA · 39.44

The Rules

Explore new worlds, turn a small deck into an engine, and race to the table’s Prestige target: 30 unless your table chose 20, 40, 50 or 60. Expand, research, build or bargain: you’ll need more than one route to the top.

Your first minute

  1. Look at your hand. Each card is a choice: convert it into a resource, or use its order.
  2. Take one action. Convert any cards you need, then use one order or offer a trade. Then the next faction acts.
  3. Keep taking turns until you pass. Passing starts your own Buy turn at once: improve your deck, then the others carry on.
  4. Build toward Prestige. Colonies, excavated relics, decisive battles and some market cards score it. Victory is checked when the round ends.

Try this: on your first turn, convert a Move for 1 Energy, then use a Construct to build a Harvester for . Your opening stock already holds its Matter and Data. A card is used one way, never both.

Reading a price: Matter Energy Life Data. A price shows one symbol per unit: is 2 Matter and is 1 of each. Four or more of one resource show the symbol once with its number, as in 5.

New to the game? Choose Learn to play on the home page or in the game menu: a practice game on the real table teaches each step (Learn to play & the table).

This rulebook describes the current playtest, named in its header. Start a new game to play these rules; a table dealt earlier keeps its archived engine and rulebook.

01 / How to win

Score Prestige (★). When a round ends with anyone at or past the table’s target, the highest score wins.

  • 3★Each ColonyWhile you hold it. A captured Colony takes its 3★ to the captor.
  • 2★Each relic you excavatePermanent, even after you use or trash the card.
  • 2★Each decisive victoryPermanent. Survive a battle and destroy every opposing unit that took part (Combat).
  • ★Printed on cardsPermanent: Monument of Accord 3 and Dyson Lattice 5 when bought; Ascendancy Codex 2 per use; Genesis Charter 2 per Colony it founds; Victory Doctrine 2 more each time it is played in a battle you win decisively.

Nothing else scores: Shipyards and ordinary purchases are worth nothing.

The Prestige target is chosen when the game is created: under Play alone, on the waiting screen when you play with others, or in New game: 20, 30, 40, 50 or 60, and 30 unless you change it. It is saved with the game and never changes.

A save made before targets existed keeps its original 40-point goal.

Victory is checked only at the end of a round, after cleanup. If anyone has reached the target, the highest Prestige wins. Reaching the target mid-round locks nothing in, and there is no round limit. Ties go to the most Colonies, then the most stored resources, then the earliest seat.

You reach 30 during Operations, but Ember Directorate ends the round on 31. Ember Directorate wins.

Select any faction’s score for its Prestige breakdown.

02 / Setup

You begin with one Colony on your home world, six cards and a small stock: no ships and no Shipyard.

The galaxy is a hex board 21 hexes across (radius 10, 331 hexes). It holds thirteen seven-hex worlds and four single-hex moons, with at least one empty hex between any two. Every home has two neutral world centers six steps away. Moons take random places, with comparable travel from every home, and never produce resources.

What everyone sees. All thirteen worlds are public from the start: terrain, resources, ownership and buildings. Moons and relic sites start hidden.

Relics. There are thirteen relic sites: one on each of the nine neutral worlds, the center included, and one on each moon. A site stays hidden until someone locates it (Exploring); arriving at its world does not. Once excavated, it is gone.

Colonize, Probe and Excavate cards come later, with units; units need a Shipyard, which you build with Construct. Battles use their own tactics (Combat). After setup you draw each new hand at the end of a round.

Your seat and your neighbor

In a solo game the four factions are dealt to the four homes at random, with an equal chance for each. Your faction keeps its name and colour, and you take the first turn. Homes pair up by halves of the map: the left and upper homes share one half, the right and lower homes the other.

Your three computer rivals keep their names, colours and profiles: Ember Directorate (Military), Violet Assembly (Economy) and Helios Compact (Expansion). New game names the rival sharing your half, for example “Your neighbor: Violet Assembly (Economy)”, and the game log repeats it.

  • Reloading or undoing never deals homes again; only a new game does.
  • The Learn to play practice game keeps a fixed starting position.
  • At a shared table, people are seated at random instead.

03 / A round

Every round runs Produce, Operations, Buy, then Cleanup & draw. Factions take one action at a time until each passes into its own Buy turn.

Where your cards go

Used cards stay in play until cleanup, whether used for an order, converted or used on your Buy turn. No draw or recovery effect can reach them: convert a Move, then play a draw-three card, and you can never draw that Move, even if your deck runs out.

  • On your own Buy turn you may upgrade or trash a card that is in play.
  • Probe keeps its card in play; Excavate, on the same card, trashes it.
  • An effect that says to discard, trash or return a card does exactly that. Drawing a card you already own, or returning one to your hand, follows the card’s text.

Resources carry over from round to round.

Your next hand arrives at cleanup

You draw your next five cards at the end of the round, so you can plan while the table finishes. Production uses no cards, and nobody plays a new hand before Operations. Whenever you must draw from an empty deck, shuffle your discard pile into a new deck. No hand is drawn after the game ends.

You finish Buy holding a Move, with a Construct in play and one card left in your deck. At cleanup both go to discard. You draw your last deck card, shuffle your discard, this round’s purchases included, into a new deck, and draw four more.

You may look through your own deck at any time: which cards remain and how many, never their order. Looking draws, shuffles and changes nothing. No other faction can inspect your deck, and replays show only its size.

Turns with nothing to decide finish for you

Each shortcut makes the only move left and changes no rule; Undo turn gives the turn back.

  • An empty Operations hand, with no trade offer awaiting your answer, passes you to Buy. (You could still have posted a trade: undo.)
  • A conversion that empties your hand on your own Operations turn passes you to Buy, unless a second build or another mandatory choice is pending. A conversion on someone else’s turn never passes the acting faction.
  • A Buy turn with no choice at all (nothing affordable, no starter to upgrade, no Buy effect, nothing to trash, no bank exchange) ends.

A notice beside your hand, such as “No cards left: moved to Buy”, stays until your next turn ends. Shared tables do the same; computer rivals and replays are unaffected.

Converting

Only level I starters convert: your six original Move and Construct cards, before you upgrade them. A Move gives 1 Energy and a Construct 1 Matter. No other card converts. Converting costs no action and draws no card; do it any time in Operations or Buy (free actions), before you start an order. A full limit never stops a conversion: the excess is lost (Resources).

Convert level I starters, beside your hand, converts every eligible starter, left to right, and leaves the rest in your hand. On a raised card, the upper-left corner uses the order and the upper-right converts.

Construct II and III: a second build

You may stop after one build, even if you could afford another. A failed second build costs nothing and keeps the choice open, and reloading keeps it too. Cancelling before the first build spends nothing. New unit cards still go to your discard.

  • If commissioning starts a battle, the battle resolves before you choose the second build. After a battle between rivals, the acting rival carries on with its own choice.
  • A Construct card’s discount applies to its first build only. World Engine builds once.

Your first build leaves you a Matter short. Accept a rival’s open offer for Matter, then pay for your second Fleet.

Construct III: draw instead of building

On your Operations turn you may play an original Construct III just to draw 1 card: no build, no payment. That spends the card and your action, and opens no second build. If you build instead, you build once, draw 1, then may pay for a second build as usual. Either way you draw at most once, and nothing if your deck and discard are both empty. No other Construct card has this choice.

05 / Resources

Four resources, four separate limits. Whatever you gain above a limit is lost at once.

Matter builds; Energy funds fleets and cards; Life commissions Settlers and founds Colonies; Data buys and improves cards. Pay the listed cost when you build or buy, one symbol per unit (reading a price). Ordinary movement costs no resources.

Limits. Each resource starts with a limit of 4. Space for one resource never holds another: 5 Matter is over a Matter limit of 4 even when you hold nothing else.

Overflow. Whenever you gain resources, anything above that resource’s limit is discarded at once, with no prompt: from production, conversions and bank exchanges alike, for every faction. The game log records the loss.

To buy a card you must be able to hold its price. A full limit is not a payment: you still pay (cards that need raised limits).

Raise your limits: the Capacity Module

Split the 4 points any way you like: all on one resource, 2 and 2, 1 on each, or anything else that adds to 4. Capacity points are not Prestige.

Your Storage pile is permanent. It lies beside your mat and keeps every module you have played, each with the split you chose. It is never drawn, shuffled, discarded or trashed, and cleanup leaves it alone. Your limit for a resource is 4, plus the points on your stored modules, plus any capacity from relics and market cards.

The price never changes: , one of each resource, for every module, however many you own. Buy several in one Buy turn if you can pay. The table shares a supply of 20; when it runs out, nobody can buy more.

  • A module is not a market card: it uses no market slot, never refills one, and takes no market discount.
  • It cannot be converted.
  • You may trash it from your hand, discard or in play on your Buy turn, but never from your Storage pile.
  • Playing it is your Operations action: capacity is never free.

Playing a module opens a selector with 1 point on each resource: change the split and confirm, or cancel to keep your action. The capacity pile shows how many you have stored.

Cards that need raised limits

The draw-two and exchange cards fit your starting limits. Many cards need 5 of one resource, so one Capacity Module is enough: Ion Thrusters costs 5. The strongest need far more: Ascendancy Codex costs 48. The card reference’s Needs limit column shows each card’s largest requirement.

To buy Ion Thrusters, buy a Capacity Module on one Buy turn and play it on a later Operations turn with at least 1 point on Energy. Then hold 5 Energy when you buy.

Where you may build

On a world whose Colony you hold, you may build on an empty, unowned hex next to a hex you already own on that world. Every hex of a seven-hex world touches its center, so in practice that means any empty hex of the world. A Colony does not give you the other hexes by itself: you claim each one by building on it. Enemy ships on the world do not take that right away.

After capturing a world’s Colony, you may replace an enemy Harvester anywhere on that world with your own: use Construct and pay a Harvester’s normal cost. The old Harvester is removed and you own its hex. Colonies, Shipyards and your own buildings are never replaced.

Moons. You may build a Shipyard on a surveyed moon where you have a ship, at its normal cost, with no Colony needed. Nothing else can be built on a moon, and a moon never produces, even with ships or a Shipyard. Ships may move and fight there.

Who owns what. A world belongs to whoever holds its center Colony; a moon to whoever owns its Shipyard. Capturing a Colony changes only the center’s owner: every other building keeps its own.

The map rings each held world or moon in its owner’s colour and draws each building in its owner’s colour. Capture Ember Directorate’s home Colony and its ring turns your colour, while Ember’s Harvester there stays orange.

07 / Moving

A Move order moves one group from one hex, at the speed of its slowest unit.

Choose a group: any number of your Settlers and Fleets on the hex, or any number of your Explorers. Explorers never travel with combat ships. Units you leave behind stay put; moving them takes another Move order on a later turn.

Speed: Settlers move 1, Fleets 2, Explorers 3. Each hex step costs 1 movement. Move II adds 1, and a movement card adds its range.

Play a Move on a hex with your units: every combat ship there starts chosen (or every Explorer, if you have no combat ship there). Untick units or lower a count to leave them behind.

Stacking: any number of your units may share a hex, within your faction limits (Building). The same holds when you commission, escape or retreat.

What stops you

The hex holdsMoving in
Enemy ships or an enemy ShipyardHostile. Stop and fight (Combat). You can never move through it.

You may also play a Move with zero movement to stay where you are. On a hostile hex that starts another battle; anywhere, it spends the card and your action. Slipstream Charts can never enter a hostile hex.

Any unit ending a move on a world or moon surveys it for everyone (Exploring). A forced retreat and a paid Explorer escape are not Move orders: a retreat takes every surviving attacker, and an escape follows the combat rules.

The corner portal

The lower-left and upper-right corners are joined by a public portal. Each has nine entrances: the corner hex and four rim hexes along each side. Each entrance leads to the matching hex straight across at the other corner, in the same order.

Crossing costs 2 movement, from an entrance to its paired exit, and no resources. You may walk to an entrance, cross, and keep moving in the same order if the group has movement left.

A Fleet standing on an entrance crosses with an ordinary Move. An Explorer can walk one step to an entrance and cross, using its 3. A Settler needs Move II or another range bonus.

  • A hostile exit stops you and starts a battle. Slipstream Charts cannot enter one.
  • Retreats and escapes use ordinary adjacent hexes, never the portal.
  • Probe distance and relic targeting ignore the portal.
  • Seeing a portal never reveals a hidden moon or relic.

Select an entrance to see its paired exit.

08 / Exploring & relics

Locate a relic with Probe, bring an Explorer to it, then Excavate it for a relic card and 2★.

What you can see

  • Worlds are always public, but seeing a world never locates its relic.
  • Surveys. Any Settler, Fleet or Explorer ending a move on a world or moon surveys it for everyone, revealing a hidden moon’s terrain. Arriving never locates a relic.
  • Units are always visible, everyone’s, even in empty space. A unit marker alone never reveals a hidden moon or relic.
  • Relic signals are private. A relic you locate stays your secret, even after later surveys, until an effect reveals it to everyone. A signal on an undiscovered moon shows you the relic’s hex, not the moon’s terrain. Other players must locate it for themselves.

Probe

Probe works from your Explorer’s current hex, wherever it is: you need not reach the world or moon first. It privately locates the nearest remaining relic you have not discovered, by ordinary hex distance, with ties broken in a fixed site order. Sites you already know are skipped; another player’s discovery does not count as yours. The Explorer stays, and the card stays in play until cleanup. Discovery relic cards work as printed.

  • If you already know every remaining relic, Probe warns you, and you keep the card and your action.
  • If every site has been excavated, you are told no relics remain, and you keep the card and your action.

When you locate the last site you did not know, a message says every remaining relic is discovered. The comms feed may say that Probe was used, never where.

Excavate

With an Explorer on the exact hex of a relic you have located, play Excavate and pay . Choose one of the three face-up relic cards and put it in your discard pile. Remove the relic from the map, gain 2 permanent Prestige, remove one of your Explorers from the site, and trash the card. Your other units there stay.

An unlocated relic cannot be excavated. Excavating is your Operations action. It changes no ownership or buildings and is not a Construct order. A failed or cancelled excavation spends nothing. Your new relic reaches your hand by the normal shuffle and draw.

The relic row. The relic deck holds 16 different cards. Three lie face up from setup, for everyone to see. A taken slot refills at once from the deck; when the deck runs out the row shrinks, and when the row is empty no more relics can be excavated. Every relic is in the card reference.

Choosing your Explorer’s site opens the relics. Press a relic’s Choose button once the site and payment are ready; you may convert first without cancelling. Cancel Excavate keeps the card.

09 / Combat

A battle is up to three volleys. In each, both sides secretly commit a tactic, reveal, and deal damage at the same time.

A battle starts when units enter a hostile hex, when a unit is commissioned at a Shipyard with enemy ships on it, or when a zero-movement Move is played on a hostile hex. Play stops, even on a rival’s turn, until every battle choice is made, and a reload resumes the waiting choice. The Move or Construct card is spent once, and its Operations turn ends once, after the battle.

Escapes

Before the first tactic, the attacker and then the defender may move some or all of their Explorers to one adjacent hex on the map free of enemy ships and Shipyards, paying per Explorer. There is no stacking limit. Ships left behind fight. Without the Energy or a safe hex, your Explorers stay. This is the only paid escape.

If every defender escapes and no defending Shipyard remains, the attacker takes the hex without a volley.

Your tactics and the military deck

In every battle each side holds 2 Concentrated Fire (+1 hit) and 2 Defensive Screen (prevent 2 hits), plus one tactic for each military card it owns. Each can be used once per battle, and all return for the next battle. Any piece type can use any tactic. Tactics are separate from your regular deck.

Military cards you buy form your military deck: never in your hand, deck or discard, never converted or trashed, never shuffled or drawn. They are public: everyone sees which each faction owns and has used. Only the tactic just committed stays secret until both reveal.

With one Rail Battery you hold 2 Fire, 2 Screen and 1 Rail Battery: five tactics for up to three volleys.

Your Military pile, beside In play, counts your military cards (in a battle, the unused ones). Select it to see them.

Commit, reveal, resolve

Each side privately commits one of its available tactics. You can see whether your opponent has committed, never what. Both are then revealed and set aside for the rest of the battle, unless War-Song recovers one.

Damage = the attack of all your surviving pieces + your tactic + any card bonuses − your opponent’s shields, never below 0. Both sides deal damage at the same time, using the pieces alive at the start of the volley. Damage stays on pieces between volleys; survivors repair fully after the battle.

Phase Lance pierces shields: your opponent’s shields prevent none of its hits that volley.

Ending a battle

A battle ends when either side has no pieces left. If defenders survive the third volley, the attackers must retreat to an adjacent hex free of enemies, their starting hex if possible. If there is none, they are destroyed. No side ever retreats automatically for being weaker.

Then come after-battle relics and the battle report, after any excess resources are discarded. A reload keeps an unread report.

Decisive victory

Gain 2 permanent Prestige when your side survives a battle and destroys every opposing unit that took part. Attacker or defender can earn it, at most once per battle.

The battle ends with…Decisive?

Each Victory Doctrine you play in a battle you win decisively adds 2 more Prestige, again if War-Song lets you replay it.

Destroyed Shipyards

Destroying a Shipyard is the only way a battle changes who owns a hex. Remove it and clear its hex’s owner, even if both sides were wiped out: the former owner stops producing there, and any faction meeting the normal requirements may build there again at the normal price (on a moon, with a ship there). The Colony and the hexes around it keep their owners. A damaged Shipyard that survives keeps its owner.

Otherwise battles leave ownership and production alone, with no occupation markers. To take a Colony, capture it (Colonies).

The bank

Give four of one resource for one of a different resource, as often as you can pay: 4 can become ; cannot. The bank needs no card and no rival, and does not use up your player trade.

Drag on your mat from one resource’s row onto another, or choose the row and then the resource to receive. Bank exchange & discards does the same: Give 4, Receive 1, Convert.

Exchange cards

Exchange Protocol, Barter Network and Clearing House each improve one resource’s bank rate to 2:1. Play one from your hand as your Operations action, or free during your own Buy turn, and choose the resource you will give. Until your Buy turn ends that round, give 2 of it for 1 of a different resource at the bank, as often as you can pay, also between your Operations actions and off turn. The card stays in play until cleanup. No rival’s rate changes.

  • A copy you have just bought enters your discard: you cannot play it this turn.
  • A second copy can improve a different resource. Choosing the same resource again never beats 2:1.
  • Unused rates also end if your Buy turn is skipped automatically.

Offers to other factions

Once per round, as your Operations action, offer 1–5 of one resource for 1–5 of a different resource. You must hold what you offer, but it is not set aside. At least one rival must still be taking Operations turns.

Each rival may accept or reject for free on its own turn. The first valid acceptance settles the trade, as long as both sides still hold the resources and have room for what they receive. You may withdraw your offer on your turn, but you cannot post another that round.

Passing does not withdraw your offer: rivals still in Operations may accept it. Every open offer expires when the last faction passes, before its Buy turn.

The Trade tab counts the offers waiting for your answer, for example “Trade (1 open)”.

11 / Your Buy turn

When you pass, you shop: buy any cards you can afford, upgrade your starters, buy capacity and trash cards you no longer want.

Buy any number of the nine face-up market cards you can afford, paying each price separately. A bought card’s slot refills at once from the same row’s deck, so you may buy the new card too. There is no purchase limit. Choose Finish Buy when you are done; you cannot return to Operations this round.

  • A bought military card joins your military deck; every other card enters your discard.
  • When a row’s deck is empty, its empty slot stays empty. No card moves in from another row.
  • A Wonder gives its Prestige the moment you buy it, and you keep it even if you trash the card later.

Late in the game the Cheap deck is empty. You buy Signal Relay: the Cheap row now shows two cards, while the other rows still refill.

Move and Construct are not for sale: your six starters are your only basic cards. Colonize and Probe / Excavate come only with units.

A card you cannot buy yet is shaded: in its price, a padlock marks a resource above your limit and a faded chip one you hold too little of. Hover over or raise it for the reason, such as Needs 2 more Data.

Three market rows

The market is three decks sorted by a card’s total printed price, each with its own row of three face-up cards. At setup, shuffle each deck and deal its row. Each deck holds two copies of each of its designs, normal and military mixed: 27 designs, 54 cards in all. A discount changes what you pay, never a card’s row.

12 / Card reference

Every card you can buy or excavate. Ids stay the same through this playtest: use them to name a card in feedback.

Your starters are 3 Move and 3 Construct, and Colonize and Probe / Excavate come with units. Needs limit is the largest limit a card’s price requires; the three draw-two cards fit your starting limits.

The card gallery shows every current market card and relic, filtered by Normal deck, Military deck or Alien relics, with market cards in their Expensive, Medium and Cheap groups. Show feedback lets you vote on a card or leave a note; they stay with the card when you filter, sort or reload, and the gallery says whether Save feedback sends them to the host or keeps them in your browser.

Card art is decoration. Each design shows a public-domain painting or print from the National Gallery of Art, Washington. The picture and its corner emblem never change what a card does: the printed name, cost and text are the card. The card art credits name every work and its artist.

Timing. An Operations relic uses your action. A battle relic is optional: play it from your regular hand, and it stays in play until cleanup.

  • Before escapes, the attacker and then the defender may play opening relics or carry on. In this window Horizon Sail can reinforce only the defender.
  • War-Song and The Silent Hour are played before your own tactic in any volley. They do not replace the tactic.
  • A recovered tactic applies its effect again when replayed, Victory Doctrine’s bonus included.
  • The Silent Hour prevents damage even when Phase Lance pierces shields.
  • After the battle, each side may use Phoenix Crucible before play resumes.
  • Declining an opening or after-battle window keeps the unused cards.
  • If a stolen Fleet was the enemy’s last unit, the battle can end without a volley once both opening windows close. Taking control earns no Prestige; destroying every remaining opposing unit still earns a decisive victory.

Targets and destinations. Choose the ships and destination before you confirm. Horizon Sail and Gravity Scepter measure ordinary hex distance, ignoring the portal and anything in between. Horizon Sail brings any number of your Fleets and Settlers together, or Explorers on their own, to your ships, Colony or Shipyard on an enemy-free hex during Operations; played in defense, it may join the battle hex. Gravity Scepter moves the whole enemy group on one hex, never onto other ships or a building. Starfarer’s Compass offers only remaining sites you have privately located; arriving surveys as usual, and never excavates or grants a relic by itself.

Prismatic Reservoir changes limits, not resources. Move any number of your existing capacity points between limits; increases and decreases must add up to zero, and every limit stays at least 4 and at least what you hold. Stored Capacity Modules keep their own splits; the Reservoir changes your effective limits. It creates no capacity and never raises a price. Draft the change, then confirm, or cancel without spending the card.

With limits of 4 Matter, 4 Energy, 4 Life and 8 Data, choose +3 Matter and −3 Data to finish at 7/4/4/5. That is legal only if you hold no more than 5 Data.

Hourglass of Two Suns gives two card actions in a row. Finish each card’s whole action, a battle or optional second build included, before the next. Free resource management stays free. The bonus turns are for cards: posting a trade offer waits for your next normal turn. Echoes of the First Dawn may return two other cards from In play, upgraded starters and relics included, but never itself, a Storage card or a card in discard. Returning Hourglass with Echoes cannot activate it twice in one round.

Buying with relics. Keepers’ Archive takes two resource units off in total, from resources in the price: 2 Matter, say, or 1 Matter and 1 Data. Oracle of the Unmade searches the hidden market decks by design, never showing their order, and takes one real remaining copy; it creates no new deck and skips no price. A military card bought either way joins your military deck. Restoring ships with Phoenix Crucible is not commissioning: it brings no Colonize or Probe cards and changes neither the battle’s outcome nor its score.

A / Learn to play & the table

Nothing here changes a rule. It shows where things are on the table and how to use them.

Learn to play

Choose Learn to play on the home page or in the game menu for a practice game, kept apart from your guest game, in which the three rivals only pass. It teaches one step at a time on the real table, starting with a Data Harvester: convert the marked Move for the missing Energy, then build with a Construct. The table and your mat come next.

  • Only the card to use next glows and gently grows (a steady glow with reduced motion); on a narrow screen your hand scrolls to it. Hover, focus or touch it for its instructions.
  • A note then lights up each part of the table. It waits until you have done what it asks, or offers Next. Between lessons it may give you cards or resources or move one of your units, and it says so.
  • Back, Skip lesson and Exit are always there. Continue Learn to play resumes where you left off. Choosing Learn to play before finishing its first step, or after the end, starts a new practice game at Lesson 1, as Restart tutorial does at any time.

Guidance. In your own games, a note beside the card, hex or button it means suggests a useful next step each turn, and explains each rule in one line the first time it matters: your first new hand, production, reshuffle, Buy turn, Shipyard, Colonize card, relic, full resource or battle. It suggests opening with a Data Harvester, then a Shipyard. It changes no rule and makes no choice for you. Switch it with Guidance on the table, above Map key (on a phone, the first of the turn’s buttons), or in the game menu; the two always agree, and this browser remembers. Learn to play keeps its own notes on.

The table

On a desktop window the whole table fits without scrolling: it shrinks to fit a small window, browser zoom makes the pieces larger or smaller, and the map takes the space that is left (overview in Start here).

  • Left column: the standings, with each faction’s public counts and its lane on the Prestige track (the acting faction’s marker glows; select a marker for its point breakdown); the round’s phases; the comms feed (select it to see it all); and the map key, with each piece’s price just after its name (faint lines part the rows) and the card icons. Prices repeat the resource symbols, by the same rule as the card corners: two Matter symbols mean 2 Matter. Each resource’s symbols sit together, in the order Matter, Energy, Life, Data: two side by side, three as a small triangle, one above two. The Map key button has the same costs, the terrain colours and full descriptions, and notices appear over the top of the key. Point at anything there for its full text.
  • Middle: the map, as large as the space allows.
  • Right edge: the supply, a drawer opened and closed with the Market tab (Escape also closes it), and kept as you leave it. Your Buy turn brings the market forward as whole cards over the map (Smaller shows the column instead), and choosing a relic to excavate opens it on the relics. The column holds six rows of three: the relics, the three market rows, then Move II, Construct II and the Capacity Module pile, and Move III, Construct III and the Trash pile.
  • Left edge: the Trade tab.
  • Bottom: the menu, your Deck, In play and Military piles, your hand, Convert level I starters (while you hold one), your mat, and Discard under the turn’s main button. Guidance, Map key and Zoom map sit at the map’s top-right corner.

No written prompt marks your turn: your hand glows and gently breathes, and screen readers say what to do. The menu holds the rules, the card gallery, a preview of every relic, a new game, Undo, playtest notes, Learn to play and Guidance. On a phone, the standings are the Table drawer and the supply is the Supply drawer, brought forward when needed; on a window taller than it is wide, the supply is a band under the map and the map key is not on the table (it would cover the map’s corner): the Map key button opens the same costs, then the full descriptions.

Your hand, piles and mat

Your hand is a fan with both upper corners of every card showing (Read a card). Cards you can use now are outlined in gold, and a card’s frame takes the colour of the resource it works with: gold for capacity, Buy and Prestige cards, red for military.

  • Raise a card by pointing, tabbing or touching: it grows to reading size and its upper corners become buttons, the order on the left and the conversion on the right. A card with no order symbols has a Use order button instead.
  • Pressing an order lights the hexes where it can start; choose one to play it there. Escape, or pressing it again, cancels.
  • Or drag the card onto a lit hex. A Move dropped on your units opens their Sector Scan, where you can leave some behind; a Construct or Colonize opens that hex’s choices. Drop it on your reserves to convert it. Dropped anywhere else, it plays nothing.

Your Deck lies face down, In play and Discard face up, each as thick as its count; select one to look through it without moving a card. Your mat shows each resource on slots 0 to 20 (Resources): a value above 20 sits on the last slot with its number, and stock above the limit shows as a red piece past the marker. Trade with the bank on the mat itself. Older saved dev games keep the earlier standings order and may open Trade as a dialog.

Reading the map

Point at a hex, or Tab to it, and the cards that could start an order there rise in your hand. Select it to open its Sector Scan: the hex’s resource and name, symbols for its owner, building, colony site, located relic and each faction’s ships, and the orders you could start there. Shortcuts are named for what moves (Move Settlers, Move Fleets, Move Settlers & Fleets, Move Explorers); you still need the card, the resources and a legal target. Each ship type shows separately, with a count under its icon when there are several. On a narrow screen, pan the map or use Zoom map.

Both tactic rows show the basic tactics and military cards, used and unused. Select a card to read it, then Commit. Selecting spends nothing, and you never see the enemy’s choice before both reveal. Max’s UI

Toggle Max’s UI in the game menu for a spacious slate-blue table against the stars. It changes only your view, and your browser remembers it. A legend under the map names its steel Matter, sapphire Energy, jade Life and amethyst Data tiles.

Select Relics, Market or Basics at the upper right to open a card bank: the face-up relics; the market rows, labelled Expensive, Moderate, Cheap (Moderate is Medium); or the upgrades, Capacity Modules and Trash. Your Buy turn opens Market and choosing an excavation site opens Relics; close a bank or press Escape to return to the map. Your resources stand as four columns of circles: inset discs for stock, coloured rings for production, faded slots past your limit.

Oracle of the Unmade shows every market design in those three rows; browse with the arrows or by swiping, and choose an affordable design with a copy left. Browsing spends nothing and shows no deck order. Keepers’ Archive uses the same cards for its face-up choices, and Echoes shows your In play cards so you can pick up to two.

Keyboard. Tab reaches every control and Enter or Space uses it; the first link skips to the player controls. Arrow keys, Home and End switch the player-control tabs, and Escape closes inspectors and reference windows.

Game reports

Export game report, beside the game log, downloads a JSON file at any time, even after victory: the saved position, rules version, factions and rival profiles, resources, point breakdowns, and the whole history from setup in readable text. The history survives reloads, and Undo keeps it, marking the range it rolled back. Exporting changes nothing in the game.

  • A save without its full history gives a partial report: nothing missing is reconstructed, and Undo into the gap marks it unavailable.
  • A solo report includes your private cards and hidden map information; replay viewers cannot export one.
  • At a shared table, the report opens only when the game ends, because it records every hidden shuffle.

For development playtests, ?dev=1 adds a Reveal entire map switch. It changes only what your screen shows: no discovery, turn, save or opponent information.

B / Playing with friends

Two to four people share a table, and computer rivals fill the other seats, so every game has four factions and the usual rules.

The host chooses Play with others, sends the link and presses Start; Standard computer rivals take the empty seats. Seats are dealt at random at the start, so friends do not always share a half of the map (homes come in pairs), and the order people joined in is not the seating. A table keeps the rules it was dealt with and races to the host’s Prestige target. Its rivals buy and play the same cards as you, Capacity Modules included (rival cards).

Everyone plays on their own view, with an account or a device the host approved, and only you act for your seat. The table service keeps the game and checks every choice, so all seats see the same board.

Scan, join, play

Choose Play with others on the home page. The waiting screen shows the link at once: share it, copy it, or let people in the room scan its QR code. It works for 24 hours. Whoever opens it enters a name and chooses how their device is used, My hand (the board is on another screen) or The whole game, then chooses Join, with no account needed; the game menu changes that choice later. You make the same choice for your own device, where The board for everyone makes it the room’s shared screen and moves your hand to your phone before you start. Compare each guest’s name and short pairing code before you let them in. Starting opens every approved player’s game, with their map, private hand and choices. Approved devices keep their seat when they reload, and More on the waiting screen replaces a lost one for the same player. Once play starts, nobody new can take a seat.

Plan, preview, confirm

On your device, tap a sector to see what your hand can do there, or choose a card first. Costs and shortfalls stay visible, and the game never converts cards for you. Every choice, off-turn conversions and exchanges included, asks you to confirm before it is sent; if the position changes first, review it again. A private card-and-sector reminder kept between turns never plays itself. Tapping the shared board only suggests a sector to the player whose input is needed.

The shared board

A shared board is optional: choose The board for everyone for your own device, or Show the board on another screen under More on the waiting screen. It takes no seat, and closing or disconnecting it never pauses the game; a disconnected player’s choices simply wait. It shows only public information: terrain, surveyed moons, ships, buildings, resources and face-up cards. A relic signal stays private until every faction knows it, and hands, deck contents and unrevealed tactics never appear, even after the game ends.

C / Computer rivals

Rivals play by your rules, prices and limits, and know only what their seat may know. Their profiles change what they want, never what they may do.

Rivals see public terrain and units plus their own relic signals: never hidden moons, unlocated relics, other hands or any deck order. Solo games, new shared tables and new AI arena matches use the same rival decisions, and a table already dealt keeps the policies it was dealt with, so an update never changes a game in progress. Each rival seat plays the profile assigned to it: Military, Economy, Expansion, or MaxBot (below). Their habits, recorded here, are not rules:

  • Score first. They take Colony and excavation chances before infrastructure, send Settlers to neutral Colony sites and, once none remain, seek captures; a Settler heads for an enemy center only with a Fleet. Explorers probe from where they stand, travel to the relic and excavate on a later draw, and stop when the rewards run out.
  • Fight with care. Fleets seek favourable fights and open Colonies for capture. Rivals gather Fleets, escort Settlers, avoid exposed hexes and guard threatened centers, portal approaches included; a fast Fleet leaves Settlers behind only where they are safe.
  • Build and plan. They upgrade starters to level III (Construct early), invest in missing Data production, and weigh Harvesters, market cards and upgrades against the time left. They prefer Construct III to II, may draw with it when no build is worth making, and may stop after one build. They keep payment ready for their next Colony, excavation, first Shipyard or urgent defence, from what they hold and what their public pieces produce, and plan shorter as someone nears the target. Their first Shipyard goes on the inward Energy hex.
  • Trade with a goal. A bank trade or exchange card must fund a concrete plan (a ready Colony or excavation, a useful build, a worthwhile card or Capacity Module) and be worth what it loses; a shortfall alone is no reason. They reconsider after every action: future production is not stock.
  • Buy what they will use. They value printed effect, price and the wait to draw a purchase; a discount counts only if a later purchase uses it. They limit duplicates, keep named cards when trimming basics, look for fights before buying more military cards, and buy Capacity Modules toward prices above their limits. On Buy turns they upgrade and trash as you can.

MaxBot. In a new solo game you may swap the Economy rival for MaxBot. It chases reusable capacity, especially Folded Warehouse, played whenever drawn (or Capacity Modules if none is available); then Harvesters, Colonies and a Shipyard on each world it holds, turning to attacks and quick Prestige as the game nears its target. It has the same rules and knowledge as any rival, and its name and strategy survive a reload.

Faster planning never gives a rival extra movement, cheaper payments or a new random choice. Tied choices keep the first card in its hand, it decides again after every change, and a blocked move costs it no card.

AI arena. Arena games use the same victory check, and an exact tie shares the rank. Only completed games earn ratings: a game that cannot finish within the arena’s limits makes its series incomplete, and none of that series counts. An unfinished game is never a win.

Card-ranking simulations only. A configured study may offer every remaining market design instead of the nine face-up cards, at printed prices and from the real supply, without showing any deck order. Ordinary games always use the three-row market.
