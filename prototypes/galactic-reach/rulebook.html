A GALAXY. FOUR FACTIONS. YOUR NEXT MOVE.

Make your mark
among the stars.

Explore new worlds, turn a small deck into an engine, and race to 20 Prestige. Expand, research, build, or bargain: you’ll need more than one route to the top.

Your first minute

  1. Look at your hand. Each card is a choice: convert it into resources, or use its order.
  2. Take one action. Convert any cards you need, then play one order or offer a trade. The next faction takes a turn.
  3. Keep taking turns until you pass. Passing starts your Buy turn immediately. Improve your deck, then let the remaining factions continue.
  4. Build toward Prestige. Colonies, fleets, relics, influence, and purchases all help. Victory is checked after the round’s vote and directive.

Try this: save a Construct card for its order; convert another card to help pay for a Scout. You cannot use the same card both ways.

This manual describes the 7.12 Pass & Buy experiment. Start a new game to try these rules. Older saved tables keep their own rules. Some printed card summaries are ahead of the implementation; the reference below records what those cards currently do.

01 / Your starting faction

Four factions share eleven seven-hex worlds on a radius-12 galaxy (469 hexes). Four home worlds touch the rim; seven inner worlds are randomized with at least one empty hex between worlds. Home worlds are visible to everyone.

You start with a central Colony, an inward-facing Shipyard, one Scout at that Shipyard, and 2 Biomass. Your ten starter cards are 3 Move, 3 Construct, 2 Colonize, and 2 Probe. Each faction also has its own shuffled 12-card military deck.

Production happens before the first five-card draw. At home, the Colony produces 1 Matter, the Shipyard 1 Energy, and its Scout another 1 Energy. Unbuilt surrounding hexes do not produce just because they belong to your home world.

02 / A round of play

  1. Produce, then draw five. Collect resources, then draw cards. Whenever your deck runs out, shuffle your discard pile to keep drawing.
  2. Operations. Starting with the first player, alternate one action: use an order, publish a trade, or pass. Converting cards and answering trades are free on your own Operations turn. Passing ends your Operations for this round and immediately starts your Buy turn.
  3. Buy. When you pass, take your own Buy turn before play moves on. Normally buy up to one card; you may also upgrade starters, decommission, and exchange relics. Finish Buy to resume Operations with the next faction that has not passed.
  4. Clean up. After the last faction finishes Buy, discard everyone’s remaining hand, check storage, and update public awards. Every third round, hold the policy vote.
  5. Choose a directive, then check victory. The first player chooses a benefit for everyone. If nobody wins, rotate the first-player marker and start the next round.

Played and converted cards go straight to discard. Resources carry between turns and rounds; keep unused cards until the shared cleanup, so automatic reactions can still protect you after your Buy turn.

03 / Resources & storage

Matter builds; Energy funds fleets and cards; Biomass founds colonies; Data buys and improves cards. Pay the listed cost when you build or buy. Ordinary movement has no resource cost.

Store 10 resources total, plus 5 per level of an unoccupied Warehouse you own. A card conversion needs room for its entire gain.

Convert all to resources checks your hand from left to right, converts every card whose full gain fits, and leaves the rest in hand. It includes Buy and reaction cards, so save those by converting individually instead. Converting a selected order cancels that selection; conversions never spend your Operations action.

Automatic gains that exceed storage are lost; when several types arrive together, storage fills in Matter → Energy → Biomass → Data order. There is no voluntary discard of stored resources in 7.12.

Each owned, unoccupied planet hex produces 1 of its resource, +1 if Rich, plus its Harvester level. A combat fleet on a planet hex also earns its faction 1 of that resource, regardless of ownership. That is one bonus per fleet, not per ship; Researchers alone earn none.

05 / Exploration & movement

★ Rich: +1 production◈ Located relic⇄ Gate connection

A Scout entering a world reveals all seven hexes to everyone. Probe signals are private. Scouts can explore without a Probe. Researchers can travel to a marked relic without revealing the surrounding world.

Select a group at one hex: all its combat ships, or its Researcher team. Move up to the slowest unit’s speed. Scouts move 2; other units move 1. You cannot split individual ships from the selected group. Your destination may contain at most five of your units, including Researchers.

Entering a hostile hex stops the movement and resolves combat. You cannot move through it. Gates connect to each other as a single movement step. Pathfinder Network avoids hostile hexes; other orders may enter them.

06 / Ships, buildings & relics

A Colony reserves its world for expansion; it does not immediately give ownership or production on every hex. Use Construct to claim an empty adjacent planet hex with a building. One building fits on a hex. Occupied hexes cannot build. A Colony cannot be replaced with a different building.

Commission combat ships at an unoccupied Shipyard you own. Recruit Researchers at your original home Colony, if you still own it, or at an unoccupied level-II structure you own. Each build or structure upgrade earns 1 Expansionist influence.

Costs use the same colored symbols as your cards and resource bar. Each symbol is labeled, so you can learn them as you play.

07 / Combat & conquest

Combat resolves automatically in up to three volleys. Each side deals one base hit per four surviving power, rounded up, then reveals a military tactic. Tactics add hits, shields, piercing, or withdrawal. Damage is simultaneous; hits go to the piece with the least remaining hull. Surviving units repair after the battle.

Each military deck contains 3 Concentrated Fire (+1 hit), 2 Defensive Screen (2 shields), 2 Flanking Run (+2 hits with a Scout or Frigate, otherwise +1), 2 Heavy Barrage (+2 with a Dreadnought or Shipyard, otherwise +1), 1 Electronic Warfare (+1 hit, 1 shield, ignore 1 enemy shield), and 2 Tactical Withdrawal (1 shield, then withdraw if able). Used tactics reshuffle when exhausted. Reactions in hand trigger automatically.

If defenders remain after the battle, the attacker attempts to retreat to a non-hostile adjacent hex. Tactical Withdrawal and a badly outmatched attacker can end combat earlier. Fleet Overdrive prevents the attacker’s early withdrawal. Researchers contribute no base power and can be destroyed.

Winning at an enemy-owned planet occupies it, stopping its owner’s production and Warehouse storage. Ownership does not transfer. To capture its central Colony, later play Colonize there with a Scout and 1 Biomass. Only the center changes owner; surrounding structures do not transfer. The captured Colony gains a conquest marker.

08 / Trade

Once per round, use your Operations action to offer 1–5 of one resource for 1–5 of a different resource. You need the offered resources, but they are not reserved. At least one rival must still be taking Operations turns.

On their own turn, each rival may accept or reject for free. The first valid acceptance settles the offer; both sides must still have the resources and enough storage after exchanging. Each gains 1 Culture influence. You may withdraw on your turn, but cannot post a second offer that round. Passing does not withdraw your open offers: rivals still in Operations may accept them, if the live balances allow it. All open offers expire when the last faction passes, before that faction’s Buy turn.

09 / Improve your deck

As soon as you pass, buy up to one basic card or one of the five face-up advanced cards. You cannot return to Operations this round. Purchases go to discard, refill the market, and earn 1 Economy influence. Basic supply is unlimited; the advanced deck has two copies of each card and recycles the market discard when needed.

During the same Buy turn you may also:

  • Upgrade starters: spend 1 Data per original starter in your hand or discard to make it level II and gain 1 Technology influence. Purchased basic cards cannot upgrade.
  • Decommission once: spend 1 Data to permanently trash any one card that was in your discard when your Buy turn began, including an original or upgraded starter. Cards still in hand, newly purchased cards, and Buy cards discarded during this turn are ineligible.
  • Use Buy cards or exchange Relics. These do not consume the normal purchase. Hold Buy cards in hand until this phase; conversions are only available in Operations.

10 / Politics & directives

Influence has four tracks: Expansionist from builds/upgrades, Culture from completed trades, Economy from purchases, and Technology from starter upgrades. Every 10 unspent points across all tracks score 1 Prestige.

Every third round, secretly bid points from a policy’s matching track. Bids are spent even if your choice loses; bidding zero is allowed. The winning policy replaces the previous one. The two pairs alternate:

  • Frontier Grants (Expansionist): first eligible build each round costs 1 less Matter; Research Stations and Colonize II’s bonus building are excluded. Open Exchange (Culture): each completed trade also gives both parties 1 Data.
  • Market Stimulus (Economy): first purchase each round costs 1 less Data. Research Mandate (Technology): starter upgrades earn 2 Technology instead of 1.

A tied vote picks the first policy when the round’s first player is seat 1 or 3, and the second when it is seat 2 or 4. After any vote, the first player chooses a directive. Storage limits apply to all gains.

Score your current holdings plus permanent purchase milestones:

Awards can change hands. A tied leader keeps an award they already hold; otherwise a tie leaves it unclaimed. Occupation alone does not remove ownership-based Prestige.

After the directive, if anyone has at least 20 Prestige, the highest Prestige wins. Break ties by most Colonies, then most stored resources. An exact tie selects the earliest seat in the browser; arena standings share that rank. Reaching 20 earlier does not lock in a win: spending influence or exchanging Relics can lower your score before the check. There is no round limit.

Automatic reactions

Keep these in hand to use them; converting one spends it. Targeting Algorithms adds 1 hit and Ablative Plating prevents 2 hits in a volley, each consuming one available copy. Additional copies can trigger in later volleys.

Emergency Jump applies only to an attacker with surviving power below 55% of the defender’s after a volley: suffer one extra hit, then attempt withdrawal. It cannot interrupt Fleet Overdrive. Salvage Crews grants Matter equal to destroyed pieces, up to three, after combat; both factions can use it.

13 / Current playtest clarifications

These are recorded behaviors, not new rules introduced by this manual. Resolving them requires a separate rules change and playtest.

Occupation, retreats, and civilian combat
  • Occupation markers currently persist after the occupying fleet leaves. They continue to block production, building, and Warehouse capacity.
  • If a retreat cannot be completed, the combat report says the fleet was lost, but the current engine leaves survivors on the contested hex.
  • Researchers have zero base power, but a Researcher-only side can still receive hits and shields from tactics. Do not treat civilians as immune to combat.
  • Combat damage ties target Researchers, Scouts, Frigates, Shipyards, then Dreadnoughts. A surviving attacker below 65% of defending power after the second volley attempts withdrawal unless using Overdrive.
  • Lost storage is trimmed automatically. Combat removes excess from the largest resource piles in order; cleanup repeatedly removes from the currently largest pile. Ties use Matter, Energy, Biomass, then Data.
