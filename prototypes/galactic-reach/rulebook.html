A GALAXY. FOUR FACTIONS. YOUR NEXT MOVE.

Make your mark
among the stars.

Explore new worlds, turn a small deck into an engine, and race to 20 Prestige. Expand, research, build, or bargain: you’ll need more than one route to the top.

Your first minute

  1. Look at your hand. Each card is a choice: convert it into resources, or use its order.
  2. Take one action. Convert any cards you need, then play one order or offer a trade. The next faction takes a turn.
  3. Keep taking turns until you pass. Passing starts your Buy turn immediately. Improve your deck, then let the remaining factions continue.
  4. Build toward Prestige. Colonies, excavated relics, and decisive combat victories score Prestige. Victory is checked when everyone finishes the round.

Try this: save a Construct card for its order; convert another card to help pay for a Settler. You cannot use the same card both ways.

This manual describes the 27.32 Cards in Play experiment. Start a new game to try these rules. Older saved tables keep their own rules. Some printed card summaries are ahead of the implementation; the reference below records what those cards currently do.

01 / Your starting faction

Four factions share thirteen seven-hex worlds and four single-hex moons on a radius-10 galaxy (331 hexes). Four home worlds touch the rim. Two neutral worlds occupy the other corners. Six neutral worlds form a symmetric ring around a seventh world at the exact center. Every home has two neutral world centers six steps away. World positions stay fixed; neutral world identities and resource tiles are shuffled each game. All thirteen worlds, including their resources, ownership, and buildings, are visible to everyone from setup. Four moons use randomized positions spread across the map, with comparable travel from every home. Moons and relic locations start hidden.

At least one empty hex separates terrain. Each of the eight neutral worlds outside the center and each moon has one relic: twelve sites total. The added center world has no relic. All worlds cover seven hexes; moons cover one and never produce resources.

You start with only a central Colony: no Settler, Explorer, Fleet, Shipyard, or other building. Your six starter cards are 3 Move and 3 Construct. There are no starting Colonize, Probe, or Excavate cards. Combat uses a separate four-card tactic hand, refreshed at the start of every battle.

Production happens before the first five-card draw. Your Colony produces 1 Matter, so you open with 1 Matter and 2 Biomass. Build a Shipyard with Construct, then commission your first units. Use the 4:1 bank to obtain Data before you have an Excavate.

02 / A round of play

  1. Produce, then draw five. Collect resources, then draw cards. Whenever your deck runs out, shuffle your discard pile to keep drawing.
  2. Operations. Starting with the first player, alternate one action: use an order, publish a trade, or pass. Converting cards and answering trades are free on your own Operations turn. Passing ends your Operations for this round and immediately starts your Buy turn.
  3. Buy. When you pass, take your own Buy turn before play moves on. Normally buy up to one card; you may also upgrade starters, decommission cards. Finish Buy to resume Operations with the next faction that has not passed.
  4. Clean up. After the last faction finishes Buy, move everyone’s remaining hand and cards in play to discard.
  5. Check victory. If nobody wins, rotate the first-player marker and start the next round.

Newly gained cards always enter your discard pile. This includes purchased cards, unit-bundled Colonize, Probe, and Excavate cards, and alien relic rewards. Draw them through normal deck circulation before using them. Drawing cards you already own and returning an existing card to hand follow their printed effects.

Keep used cards in play until the shared round cleanup. This includes cards used for an order, converted for resources, used during Buy, or triggered as reactions. They are outside your hand, deck, and discard, so later draws and discard-recovery effects cannot pick them up again this round. For example, converting Move and then playing a draw-three card cannot draw that Move, even if your deck empties. Only the discard pile reshuffles. At cleanup, discard your cards in play together with your remaining hand. An effect that explicitly discards, trashes, or returns a card is an exception: successful Colonize, Probe, and Excavate orders are still permanently trashed. Newly gained cards still enter discard. Keep unused reactions in hand until they trigger or cleanup occurs. Resources carry between rounds.

03 / Resources & storage

Under each available resource amount, +X shows what your current planets, Harvesters, and combat fleets would produce each round. It updates when control, buildings, or ships change. Occupied planets do not produce for their owner. Explorers alone and moons produce nothing. This is the full gain before choosing any excess-storage discards; card conversions and one-time rewards are not included.

Matter builds; Energy funds fleets and cards; Biomass founds colonies; Data buys and improves cards. Pay the listed cost when you build or buy. Ordinary movement has no resource cost.

Start with space for 10 resources total. During your own Operations or Buy turn, pay to permanently add 5 storage. Your first paid upgrade costs 1 of each resource, your second costs 2 of each, your third costs 3 of each, and so on. Count your own paid upgrades only. Free storage from a relic does not increase this price. For example, after one paid upgrade and one free relic upgrade, you have 20 storage and your next paid upgrade costs 2 Matter, 2 Energy, 2 Biomass, and 2 Data. Repeat while you can afford it. This is a free resource-management action: it needs no card, map hex, building, or purchase, and preserves your current order. Occupation does not reduce this capacity. Collect the full resource gain, then resolve any excess storage before taking another action.

Convert all to resources checks your hand from left to right and pauses when a conversion exceeds storage. Choose what to discard before converting more cards. It includes Buy and reaction cards, so save those by converting individually instead. Converting a selected order cancels that selection; conversions never spend your Operations action.

Whenever a gain takes you over your storage limit, choose enough resources to discard to return to capacity. You may discard resources you already held or resources you just collected. Play pauses for your choice, including gains from Production, card conversions, relics, and combat. Reloading preserves the resources awaiting your choice. Computer rivals choose their own discards. During your own Operations or Buy turn, you may discard any number of stored resources to make room. Discarding is free, gives nothing in return, and does not spend a card or end your turn.

Combat salvage is collected in full by both sides. Each faction resolves its own excess resources before further turns continue.

Moons never produce resources. Rich planets and their star markers are removed. Each owned, unoccupied resource planet hex produces 1 of its resource, plus its Harvester level. A combat fleet on a planet hex also earns its faction 1 of that resource, regardless of ownership. That is one bonus per fleet, not per ship; Explorers alone earn none.

05 / Exploration & movement

◈ Located relic● Gray moon · no production

World terrain is always public, but seeing a world does not locate its relic. Any Settler, Fleet, or Explorer ending a move surveys that world or moon, revealing its terrain to everyone. Arrival never locates a relic. Use Probe from your Explorer’s current hex to locate the nearest unlocated remaining relic privately, even on an unvisited world or hidden moon. You do not need to reach that world or moon first. A signal on an undiscovered moon reveals the relic’s hex to you without revealing the moon’s terrain. Other players must locate it for themselves. Existing discovery relic cards keep their printed effects. Probe and Chorus signals stay private after later terrain surveys. Only an effect that explicitly reveals relics to everyone makes them public. All players’ units remain visible, including in empty space. Unit markers alone do not reveal a hidden moon or relic.

Read each ship type separately on the map. A number beneath a ship icon is the count of that type; an icon without a number means one. A building icon is separate from the ships. Select the hex to read every faction’s exact units in Sector Scan. On a narrow screen, pan the map or use Zoom map for a closer view.

Select a group at one hex: all its combat ships, or its Explorer team. Move up to the slowest unit’s speed. Settlers move 1 hex, Fleets 2 hexes, Explorers 3 hexes. Move II adds 1 hex to that order. A group uses its slowest unit’s speed: a Settler and Fleet together move 1 hex. Explorers travel as a separate civilian team and use Excavate to recover relics. You cannot split individual ships from the selected group. Your destination may contain at most five of your units, including Explorers.

Entering a hostile hex stops the movement and resolves combat. You cannot move through it. Pathfinder Network avoids hostile hexes; other orders may enter them.

06 / Ships, buildings & relics

A Colony reserves its world for expansion; it does not immediately give ownership or production on every hex. Use Construct to claim an empty adjacent planet hex with a building. One building fits on a hex. Occupied hexes cannot build. A Colony cannot be replaced with a different building. Shipyards have one level and cannot be upgraded. Only Harvesters can upgrade to level II.

Commission all units at an unoccupied Shipyard you own. Commission Explorers only at an unoccupied Shipyard you own. Colonies and other structures cannot commission them.

Costs use the same colored symbols as your cards and resource bar. Each symbol is labeled, so you can learn them as you play.

Excavate a relic

Browse the face-up rewards in the Relics tab, beside Hand, Market, Trade, and Fleet. Starting Excavate with your Explorer’s relic site selected, or selecting that site while Excavate is active, opens Relics automatically. Choose buttons stay disabled until the site and payment are ready. You can return to Hand to convert resources, then return to Relics; this does not spend or cancel the selected order. Use Cancel Excavate to keep the card and choose a different order. Ordinary map inspection does not change tabs.

First locate the relic with Probe or a discovery relic card. Then move your Explorer onto its exact hex and play Excavate. An unlocated relic cannot be excavated. Pay 1 Matter, 1 Energy, and 2 Data. Choose one of the three face-up alien relic cards and put it in your discard pile. Remove the relic from the map, gain 2 permanent Prestige, and immediately trash the used Excavate card. Remove one of your Explorers from the site. Other units there remain. Failed or canceled excavation choices spend no resources, cards, or units. Excavation uses your Operations action, leaves ownership and buildings unchanged, and cannot be discounted by Construct II. The reward enters normal deck circulation: shuffle it into your deck when needed, then draw it before using it.

Research Stations are removed. Moons are gray, produce no resources even with a combat fleet, and cannot be colonized, built on, or terraformed. Ships may move and fight there.

The shuffled alien relic deck has 12 unique cards, one of each. Reveal a shared row of three cards at setup. After every excavation, refill the chosen slot immediately from the deck. When the deck runs out, the row shrinks; when the row is empty, excavation is unavailable. Everyone can inspect the row. Browse the alien relic cards below or use the in-game preview.

07 / Combat & conquest

Choose a tactic in each of up to three volleys. Every battle starts each side with 2 Concentrated Fire (+1 hit) and 2 Defensive Screen (prevent 2 hits). These four temporary tactics are separate from your regular deck. There is no military draw pile, shuffle, or replacement draw. Every ship type can use either tactic.

Escape before combat

Before either side chooses its first tactic, the attacker and then the defender may move some or all of their Explorers to one adjacent, enemy-free hex. Pay 1 Energy per escaping Explorer. The destination must be on the map and must fit within the five-unit limit. Ships left behind fight. Without enough Energy or a safe destination, choose to stay. This is the only paid Explorer escape window. If every defender escapes and no defending Shipyard remains, the attacker takes the hex without a volley.

Commit, reveal, resolve

Each side privately commits one of its available tactics. You can see whether your opponent has committed, but never their choice before you commit. Once both have chosen, reveal both cards and remove them from this battle’s hand. A used card cannot be chosen again. Public used tactics and remaining card counts help you plan later volleys. Each new battle restores all four cards.

Add the attack of all your surviving pieces, your chosen tactic, and applicable card bonuses. Subtract the opponent’s shields to find the damage they receive, to a minimum of zero. Both sides deal damage simultaneously, using the pieces alive at the start of the volley. Hits target the piece with the least remaining hull; ties target Explorers, Settlers, Fleets, then Shipyards. Damage stays on pieces between volleys. For example, one Fleet using Concentrated Fire deals 3 + 1 − 2 = 2 damage to an opponent using Defensive Screen. Reactions held in your regular hand still trigger automatically at their printed timing.

If either side has no pieces left, the battle ends. If defenders survive the third volley, the surviving attacker must retreat to an adjacent, enemy-free hex with enough unit capacity, preferring its origin when legal. If no retreat can fit, those attacking survivors are destroyed. Emergency Jump may end a battle earlier as described in the card reference; there is no automatic retreat based only on relative attack strength. Surviving pieces repair fully after the battle. Combat pauses normal play, including during a rival’s turn, until all choices are resolved; reloading resumes the pending choice. The moving order is spent once and its Operations turn ends once, after the battle. Review the automatic final report before normal play resumes. Resolve any excess-resource discard first. Reloading also preserves a report awaiting acknowledgment.

Decisive combat victory: gain 1 permanent Prestige when your side survives a fight and destroys every opposing unit that took part. Either the attacker or defender may earn it. The enemy must have brought at least one Settler, Explorer, or Fleet; destroying an undefended Shipyard alone earns no combat Prestige. If any opposing unit escapes or retreats, gain no point, even if you destroy the units left behind. A mutual wipe earns neither side a point. Enemy survivors destroyed because no retreat is possible count as destroyed. Each fight awards at most one point.

Winning at an enemy-owned planet occupies it, stopping its owner’s production. Ownership does not transfer. To capture its central Colony, later play Colonize there with a Settler and 1 Biomass. Only the center changes owner; surrounding structures do not transfer. The captured Colony gains a conquest marker.

08 / Trade

Open Bank exchange & discards beneath your resources. Choose one resource button under Give 4 and one under Receive 1, then click Convert. Selecting buttons spends nothing. Convert becomes available when the two types differ, you hold at least four of the resource to give, and bank exchanges are allowed on your turn. Keep the selections to repeat the exchange while you can pay.

Bank exchange, 4:1. During your own Operations or Buy turn, give four of one resource to gain one of a different resource. For example, 4 Energy can become 1 Data; 2 Energy plus 2 Matter cannot. Repeat while you can pay. Bank exchanges are free, require no card or rival. They do not use your once-per-round player trade.

Trade Consortium is a card order with a better rate: choose a free resource or up to two 2:1 exchanges. You choose every resource; see the card reference.

Once per round, use your Operations action to offer 1–5 of one resource for 1–5 of a different resource. You need the offered resources, but they are not reserved. At least one rival must still be taking Operations turns.

On their own turn, each rival may accept or reject for free. The first valid acceptance settles the offer; both sides must still have the resources and enough storage after exchanging. You may withdraw on your turn, but cannot post a second offer that round. Passing does not withdraw your open offers: rivals still in Operations may accept them, if the live balances allow it. All open offers expire when the last faction passes, before that faction’s Buy turn.

09 / Improve your deck

Click Deck to inspect the cards remaining in your draw pile. Cards are grouped by name, starter status, and level, with quantities. Their draw order is hidden. Opening this view does not draw, shuffle, or change cards. Other factions cannot inspect your deck. Replays show deck size only.

Your player mat keeps Deck, In play, and Discard as separate piles beside your hand. Click a pile to inspect it. In play holds cards you have already used this round; Discard holds cards available for a later reshuffle, including newly gained cards. Inspecting a pile never moves its cards. Choose an order or conversion using the buttons on a card in your hand. Selecting a map order brings the board into view; use Your cards to return. Passing brings your Buy choices into view.

As soon as you pass, buy up to one Move or Construct card, or one of the five face-up advanced cards. Colonize, Probe, and Excavate are bundled only with unit commissions. You cannot return to Operations this round. Purchases go to discard and refill the market. Basic supply is unlimited; the advanced deck has two copies of each card and recycles the market discard when needed.

During the same Buy turn you may also:

  • Upgrade starters: spend two of one chosen resource type per original starter in your hand or discard to make it level II. Purchased basic cards and cards currently in play cannot upgrade.
  • Decommission cards: spend three of one chosen resource type per card to permanently trash as many cards as you can afford from your discard when your Buy turn began, including an original or upgraded starter. Cards still in hand or in play, newly purchased cards, and cards explicitly discarded during this turn are ineligible.
  • Use Buy cards. These do not consume the normal purchase. Hold Buy cards in hand until this phase; conversions are only available in Operations.

10 / End of the round

When everyone has finished Buy, move all remaining hands and cards in play to discard, then check victory. Then rotate the first-player marker and begin the next round with Production and Draw. The council directive and its resource bonuses are removed.

11 / Prestige & victory

Click any faction’s point total to see its Prestige breakdown. Score each Colony, plus permanent relic and combat achievements:

SourcePrestige
Colonies3 each, with no pair bonus. Five Colonies score 15.
Excavated relics2 permanent Prestige per relic you excavated, even after using or trashing its card.
Decisive combat victories1 permanent Prestige per fight won by destroying all opposing units. No point if any opposing unit escapes or retreats; see Combat & conquest.

Shipyards and card purchases award no Prestige. For example, one Colony, four Shipyards, and sixteen purchased cards still score just 3 Prestige before relics or combat. Occupation alone does not remove ownership-based Prestige.

After everyone finishes Buy and the round is cleaned up, if anyone has at least 20 Prestige, the highest Prestige wins. Break ties by most Colonies, then most stored resources. An exact tie selects the earliest seat in the browser; arena standings share that rank. Reaching 20 earlier does not lock in a win: finish the round before checking the standings. There is no round limit.

For example, if you reach 20 during Operations but Ember Directorate finishes the round with 21, the result is “Ember Directorate wins.” When your faction wins, the message reads “You win.”

The goal is 20 Prestige. Your starting Colony is worth 3 points. Six Colonies and one relic score 20; five Colonies, two relics, and one decisive combat victory also score 20. Relic and combat Prestige survive later losses and are saved with your faction’s score. These scoring rules are retained from 23.28. New 27.32 games permanently trash successfully used Colonize and Probe cards and retain the storage-planning rivals; older tables keep their pinned behavior.

Computer rivals follow the same costs, card limits, scoring, and victory check. They use public ships and structures to avoid exposed Settler moves, gather Fleets, and respond to nearby threats. They can use their own cards and private Probe results, but cannot inspect another faction’s hand or unlocated relics. Their strategy profile changes their priorities, not their legal actions.

AI arena games use this same victory check. Only completed games earn placement ratings. Simulations have time and decision limits. If any game cannot finish within those limits, the arena reports an incomplete series and none of its games change ratings. Retry with another series; an unfinished game is never counted as a victory.

Game reports

Click Export game report beside the Game log to download a JSON file at any time, including after victory. It contains the saved position, rules version, faction and computer-rival profiles, resources, point breakdowns, and a chronological log with readable text.

New games retain their history from setup through victory, including resource changes, card gains and conversions, construction, movement, public surveys and private relic signals, trades, excavation, combat choices and outcomes, and scoring. Reloading keeps that history in both browser saves and account-backed tables. The Comms Feed shows only recent entries; it does not limit the exported log. Undo keeps the audit trail and adds a boundary identifying the events rolled back.

If a save has no matching full history, its report is marked partial and includes only the history still available. Missing past actions cannot be reconstructed. If Undo reaches into missing history, its earlier event range is marked unavailable. Starting a new game starts a new complete history.

The report includes private cards and hidden map information from your solo game. Exporting downloads a local file and changes no cards, resources, random draws, or turns. Replay viewers keep their recorded perspective and do not export private solo-game reports.

13 / Current playtest clarifications

Computer rivals pursue Prestige with the same cards, costs, and legal actions as you. They take available Colony and excavation opportunities before adding infrastructure. Every profile sends Settlers toward neutral Colony sites. Explorers use Probe from their current hex, permanently trash the used card, then travel to the privately located relic. They do not wait on a world or moon to search it. Fleets seek favorable fights or open enemy Colonies for capture. Explorers pursue located relics and wait for Probe if no signal is known; they stop when the shared relic rewards are exhausted. Rivals see public terrain and units and their own private relic signals. They cannot consult hidden moon positions, unlocated relic sectors, enemy hands, or deck order.

Rivals keep useful Move and Construct cards in circulation and preserve Colonize, Probe, and Excavate for their orders. They may skip a purchase, storage upgrade, or extra building to keep cards and resources for scoring.

Rivals plan storage using their current resources and the production their public ships and structures would earn. They keep payment for their next Colony and excavation, and for a first Shipyard when needed. They save a feasible mix for a worthwhile storage purchase and can buy capacity during Operations or before other Buy spending. They may buy before converting a card that would overflow. They shorten their planning horizon as a faction approaches victory and skip expensive upgrades unlikely to repay their cost. Forecasts use no hidden cards or future enemy decisions. Rivals pay the same escalating prices and cannot upgrade during an overflow choice. When converting cards to fund an order, they retain its payment during any overflow choice.

They avoid destinations exposed to nearby stronger Fleets, gather Fleets, escort threatened Settlers, and move toward safer hexes when outmatched. They upgrade Construct starters early and invest in missing Data production. They reinforce against visible threats and keep seeking captures when no neutral worlds remain. Military, Economy, and Expansion profiles favor different routes, but all pursue points. Their first Shipyard still uses the inward Energy hex, and equal-distance paths use their home’s orientation. A blocked or illegal move costs no card. The rival may then choose another action or convert a card normally.

These are recorded behaviors, not new rules introduced by this manual. Resolving them requires a separate rules change and playtest.

Occupation, retreats, and civilian combat
  • Occupation markers currently persist after the occupying fleet leaves. They continue to block production and building.
  • Attacking survivors that cannot retreat are destroyed, matching the report.
  • Explorers and Settlers may attack. Both contribute 1 attack and use either basic tactic. War-Song and Obelisk’s offensive bonus require a surviving Fleet.
  • Combat damage ties target Explorers, Settlers, Fleets, then Shipyards. The old power-ratio withdrawal and random military deck are removed.
  • Purchased storage capacity is permanent. Occupation and combat never reduce it; collect excess resources in full and choose the discards that bring you back to capacity.
